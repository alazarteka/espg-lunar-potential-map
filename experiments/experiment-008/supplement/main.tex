\begin{abstract}
We prospectively audited whether the Lunar Prospector electron-reflectometer
sampling geometry can identify a global, time-dependent lunar potential field.
No fitted potential, fit-quality flag, beam estimate, or spacecraft-potential
estimate entered the analysis.  Each structurally valid, surface-connected
15-row energy sweep was granted an oracle-known scalar spacecraft-relative
potential, making the experiment an optimistic upper bound on information in
any later selected measurement set.  The primary model comprised independent
24-hour real spherical-harmonic fields through degree $\ell=5$ (36 parameters
per day).  All 560 daily operators were algebraically full rank, but none was
physically stable: the median sampled-RMS/global-RMS visibility of the weakest
mode was $4.5191538242914006\times10^{-5}$, and all 560 windows failed the
frozen 25-V recovery gate.  In contrast, prospectively declared 72-hour
$\ell=1$, 168-hour $\ell\leq3$, one-synodic-period $\ell\leq10$, whole-mission
static $\ell\leq20$, and whole-mission constant-plus-synodic $\ell\leq10$
classes passed their applicable stability and recovery gates.  The frozen
decision tree therefore yields \textbf{Outcome C}: the submitted daily
$\ell=5$ class fails, while specified coarse, static, and shared-synodic
classes are conditionally identifiable from the design.  This is not recovery
of absolute lunar surface potential.  Measurement validation remains open,
and an unknown additive spacecraft potential is exactly confounded with the
spatial monopole and its temporal counterparts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background and scope}

Lunar Prospector sampled along a moving orbital track rather than observing the
whole lunar surface simultaneously.  A regularized spherical-harmonic fit can
always return a smooth map, but smoothness does not establish that the data
constrain that map.  Experiment 008 therefore asks a narrower question: given
the actual sampling times and corrected magnetic-field surface intersections,
which declared spatial and spatiotemporal field classes are identifiable?

The protocol, configurations, seeds, model grid, thresholds, and outcome tree
were frozen before any scientific singular spectrum or recovery result was
opened.  Two independent pre-outcome audits found that empirical column
normalization, an unweighted latitude--longitude grid norm, midpoint temporal
evaluation, Gram-matrix rank decisions, and 32-draw tail estimates could give
misleading answers.  Prospective Amendment 03 replaced them with a physical
field metric, exact row-level temporal products, direct null-space checks,
tall-skinny QR (TSQR), and a 16,384-draw covariance gate.  It also made the
spacecraft-potential gauge and the outcome precedence explicit.  The original
32-replicate injections and empirical column-normalization diagnostics were
retained as labeled checks rather than discarded.

The information barrier allowed the 560 raw Level-1 ER/MAG files, attitude
history, SPICE kernels, and geometry/harmonic code.  It prohibited fitted
potentials and all selections derived from them.  Thus, a failure here is an
upper-bound sampling result: removing sweeps after fit-quality selection cannot
add geometric information.  A success here is only a design result and does
not validate a measurement.

\section{Estimand, observation unit, and gauge}

The canonical estimand grants sweep $i$ one noisily observed scalar relative
potential and represents a declared field by coefficients $c$:
\begin{equation}
  y_i = A_i c + \epsilon_i, \qquad
  \epsilon_i\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2).
  \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
This is an oracle measurement model, not a claim that the loss-cone analysis
has already delivered such a scalar.  One sweep contains 15 energy rows, but
those rows share one fitted potential.  The observation operator consequently
has one row per sweep,
\begin{equation}
 A_{i,qk}=\frac{1}{n_i}\sum_{j\in i}
 T_q(t_{ij})R_k(x_{ij}),
 \label{eq:operator}
\end{equation}
where the sum includes only surface-connected row footprints.  The midpoint
UTC used to assign a sweep to a window is computed from all 15 structurally
valid UTC rows.  Equation~\eqref{eq:operator} evaluates the temporal--spatial
product at every connected row; it is not
$T_q(t_{i,\mathrm{mid}})\,\overline{R_k(x_i)}$.

The scalar available to a surface-connected electron measurement is
spacecraft-relative.  If an additive spacecraft potential $u(t)$ is unknown,
then adding offset columns to Equation~\eqref{eq:model} duplicates the spatial
$\ell=0$ column and, for a temporal model, its matching temporal columns.  The
augmented absolute-potential problem is therefore exactly rank deficient in
those directions.  We report both the optimistic oracle estimand retaining
$\ell=0$ and a gauge-safe contrast estimand with every $\ell=0$ column removed.
Neither can justify the phrase ``recovered absolute surface potential.''

\subsection{Frame mismatch discovered before outcomes}

Static code inspection found a production coordinate-frame defect distinct
from the earlier next-sample attitude lookup.  The production path rotated the
spacecraft-to-Sun vector from J2000 to \texttt{IAU\_MOON}, then passed it with a
J2000 attitude-derived spin axis to a rotation builder whose two inputs are
defined to be J2000 vectors.  Projecting one vector against another expressed
in a different frame is not a valid coordinate operation.  Amendment 01 froze
four variants before operator outcomes: canonical frame-consistent geometry;
frame-consistent geometry with legacy next-sample attitude; corrected attitude
with the frame-mixed production path; and the submitted combination of legacy
attitude plus frame mixing.

On the pre-outcome one-day fixture, the frame-mixed implementation reproduced
production with 9,180 valid projected rows, zero validity or polarity
disagreements, and maximum latitude and longitude discrepancies of
$2.2737367544323206\times10^{-13}$ and
$5.115907697472721\times10^{-13}$ degrees.  Relative to canonical geometry,
the production frame mix moved common footprints by 47.8247 km median,
194.768 km at the 95th percentile, and 419.166 km maximum.  The attitude-only
contrast was 1.06186, 9.75790, and 85.2621 km; the submitted combined legacy
contrast was 47.9543, 194.283, and 417.739 km.  The defect is therefore material
to production footprints and connectivity, although, as shown below, it does
not change the primary identifiability verdict.

\section{Real harmonics and the physical field metric}

Columns are degree-major and order-minor, $m=-\ell,\ldots,+\ell$.  From
SciPy's orthonormal complex harmonics $Y_{\ell m}$ with the Condon--Shortley
phase, the real basis is
\begin{equation}
R_{\ell m}=\begin{cases}
 \sqrt{2}(-1)^{|m|}\operatorname{Im}Y_{\ell,|m|}, &m<0,\\
 Y_{\ell0}, &m=0,\\
 \sqrt{2}(-1)^m\operatorname{Re}Y_{\ell m}, &m>0.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:realbasis}
\end{equation}
The implementation was tested for numerical orthonormality and for field and
coefficient-norm parity with the repository's complex representation.  A
degree limit $L$ has $p=(L+1)^2$ spatial coefficients.

For $f(x)=\sum_k c_kR_k(x)$, the analytic surface-area mean-square metric is
\begin{equation}
 \lVert f\rVert_{\mathrm{global}}^2
 =\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S^2}f(x)^2\,d\Omega
 =c^T G_xc,\qquad G_x=\frac{I}{4\pi}.
 \label{eq:spatialmetric}
\end{equation}
This supersedes the original unweighted equal-angle grid, which overweights
polar samples.  An area-weighted 2-degree grid was used as a discretization
check; the unweighted grid remains only a legacy replay.

The shared synodic model uses
$T(t)=(1,\cos\omega t,\sin\omega t)$ with period 29.530588 days.  Its metric is
$G=G_t\otimes G_x$, where $G_t$ is the analytic continuous-time average of
$T(t)T(t)^T$ over the half-open interval from the earliest complete-sweep UTC
through one nanosecond beyond the latest.  Static and independent-window
models use $G_x$.

Let $d=G^{1/2}c$ be metric coordinates, so $\lVert d\rVert_2$ is global RMS.
The primary decomposition is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde A=\frac{A G^{-1/2}}{\sqrt n}=U\Sigma V^T.
 \label{eq:metricoperator}
\end{equation}
Each singular value is consequently a sampled-RMS/global-RMS visibility
ratio.  A right singular vector maps to physical coefficients through
$G^{-1/2}$.  This makes a weak column or weak combination physically visible
in the spectrum instead of scaling it away by empirical unit-column
normalization.

\section{Numerical audit and decision rules}

Independent-window operators were decomposed by direct float64 SVD.  Large
whole-mission operators were streamed in chunks: each chunk received a reduced
QR factorization; stacked $R$ factors were repeatedly reduced; and the final
small $R/\sqrt n$ received a direct SVD.  This TSQR construction preserves the
tall matrix's singular spectrum without squaring its condition number.  Gram
matrices were not used for rank or effective-rank decisions.

Exact numerical rank used
$\max(n,p)\epsilon_{64}\sigma_{\max}$.  Effective ranks used relative
thresholds $10^{-3}$, $10^{-6}$, and $10^{-9}$.  A window was unstable if any
of the following held: exact rank below $p$; condition number at least $10^6$;
effective rank at $10^{-6}$ below $p$; or minimum physical visibility at most
0.1.  A 100-V global-RMS weakest mode is a material near-null witness when at
least 50\% of eligible windows have visibility at most 0.1.  Nonempty epoch-
anchored half-open windows were eligible; empty scheduled windows were reported
separately, and a nonempty underdetermined window was an attempted failure.

The primary class passes only if at least 90\% of windows are stable, at least
90\% pass recovery, and no material witness exists.  Outcome B requires at
least 50\% primary instability, at least 50\% recovery failure, and a material
witness.  Outcome C takes precedence over B when all B criteria hold and at
least one prospectively declared coarse or static class passes the analogous
90\% gates without a material witness.  Provenance, geometry, or numerical
stop-rule failure would instead force Outcome D.

\section{Recovery, challenge injections, and blocked tests}

\subsection{Frozen recovery gate}

For a full-rank unregularized estimator with independent sweep noise, the
metric-coordinate estimation error is independent of injected truth.  If
$s_j$ are the singular values in Equation~\eqref{eq:metricoperator}, its
covariance eigenvalues are $\sigma^2/(n s_j^2)$.  The outcome gate evaluated
this exact covariance with 16,384 deterministic PCG64DXSM standard-normal
draws per parameter count at $\sigma=25$ V.  Relative global error was divided
by a 100-V truth RMS.  A window passed only when its median error was below
0.25 and its 90th percentile was below 0.5; rank-deficient windows failed
automatically.

The original explicit trials remained an implementation check: 32 PCG64
realizations in every window at 10, 25, 50, and 100 V, with degree power
proportional to $(\ell+1)^{-2}$ and every truth scaled to 100 V global RMS.
Equal-total-power degree shells and the weakest metric mode challenged spectral
dependence.  Recoveries included unregularized SVD, relative truncated SVD at
$10^{-3}$, $10^{-6}$, and $10^{-9}$, and ridge penalties $10^{-6}$,
$10^{-3}$, 1, and $10^3$ after empirical column normalization.  Regularized
answers are bias--variance diagnostics and cannot establish unregularized
identifiability.

Six out-of-basis great-circle Gaussian caps used centers $(0,0)$, $(60,0)$,
and $(-60,180)$ degrees and angular FWHM 15 and 30 degrees.  Their errors were
decomposed into best-$L=5$ approximation bias and estimation error.  These cap
tests were prospectively descriptive and do not enter Outcomes A--D.

\subsection{Blocked synthetic recovery}

Blocked tests generated observations before splitting and kept complete
sweeps together.  Whole-mission static and constant-plus-synodic $L=5$ models
used a final-25\%-of-time holdout, twelve 30-degree longitude holdouts, and four
interleaved seven-day-block folds.  Daily independent fields instead used a
contiguous final-quarter holdout within each day and every available
30-degree longitude sector.  All blocked tests are descriptive: the mission-
tail test is not meaningful for unrelated daily coefficient vectors, and
success on matched synthetic truths does not show that the real field belongs
to the shared temporal class.

\section{Results}

No provenance, geometry, replay, or numerical stop rule was triggered.  The
canonical geometry cache contained 399,643 connected sweeps, and the primary
calendar-day partition had 560 nonempty windows and zero scheduled empty
windows.

\section{Outcome and interpretation}

For the primary daily $L=5$ model, the unstable fraction, failed-recovery
fraction, and material-witness fraction are all exactly 1.0.  It therefore
satisfies every frozen Outcome B criterion.  However, multiple prospectively
specified classes meet the at-least-90\% stability and recovery requirements
without a material witness: 72-hour $L=1$; 168-hour $L\leq3$; one-synodic-
period $L\leq10$; whole-mission static $L\leq20$; and whole-mission shared
constant-plus-synodic $L\leq10$.  Under the frozen precedence rule, the final
classification is therefore \textbf{Outcome C}, not B.

Outcome C is a boundary statement.  The daily $L=5$ model is algebraically
full rank but physically non-identifiable at the frozen 100-V field and 25-V
sweep-noise scales.  Longer independent windows and tightly shared temporal
bases can become conditionally identifiable because they pool orbital coverage
over time.  Such pooling changes the estimand: it does not reconstruct an
instantaneous global map.

\section{Limitations}

First, the experiment never reads a measured potential.  Its iid Gaussian
noise levels are controlled reference scales, not an empirical error model for
loss-cone fits.  Real errors may be heteroscedastic, correlated, biased, and
selected by environment or fit quality.  The oracle inclusion of every
connected sweep makes failures strong but successes conditional.

Second, the spacecraft-potential gauge is unresolved.  Absolute $\ell=0$
surface potential and corresponding temporal terms cannot be separated from
an unknown additive spacecraft potential using this operator alone.  The
contrast checks remove the exact gauge direction but do not supply a physical
spacecraft-potential calibration.

Third, all passing models assume their declared band limit and temporal class.
The localized-cap tests show severe aliasing when truth lies outside the daily
$L=5$ basis.  Passing matched-truth blocked tests for a static or shared
synodic model do not establish that the Moon obeys either model.

Fourth, canonical results correct a material production frame mismatch.  Any
previous product using the mixed-frame path requires separate reassessment of
footprints, connectivity, polarity, and illumination.  The robustness of the
identifiability verdict across frame variants does not validate those physical
locations.

Finally, the pass/fail thresholds are prospective engineering definitions of
usable visibility and noisy recovery.  The phrase ``physical non-
identifiability'' here means failure under those declared metrics; it does not
erase the mathematical fact that every primary matrix has nonzero singular
values in finite precision.

\section{Conclusions}

Actual Lunar Prospector geometry does not support the submitted independent
daily degree-5 global field.  Full numerical rank alone is misleading: a
100-V global weakest mode produces only $1.085\times10^{-4}$ to 0.0405 V RMS
on a day's sampled footprints, and 25-V noise drives global recovery errors
many times the truth amplitude.  This failure survives every frozen geometry,
gauge, reducer, longitude, and row-weighting sensitivity.

The same geometry does support prospectively defined coarse independent
windows, mission-integrated static fields, and a mission-shared
constant-plus-synodic basis.  Those successes establish conditional design
identifiability and motivate sharply constrained summaries, not an
instantaneous global potential map.  Until the scalar measurement itself and
the spacecraft-potential contribution are independently validated, Experiment
008 supports no claim of recovered physical lunar surface potential.
