## 0.2 Detailed mathematical adjudication

### 0.2.1 Findings 1 and 2: observable experiment and endogenous state

The correction is structural. Write

\[
S_i=(S_i^{\rm obs},S_i^{\rm lat}),\qquad
Z_i=(Z_i^{\rm obs},Z_i^{\rm lat}),
\]

and further split recorded state into a proved-exogenous design component \(D_i\) and a possibly endogenous component \(E_i\). The observable is not \((C,S,Z)\) with an ambiguous \(S\); it is the random element specified in Section 3.5. If \(E_i\) is generated from current or prior counts, the valid likelihood is a joint likelihood such as

\[
P_{u,\nu}(E_i=e,C_i=c,R_i=r\mid D_i=d),
\]

not \(P(C_i=c\mid E_i=e,D_i=d)\) combined with an unchanged unconditional Poisson law. The counterexample \(K\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)\), \(E=1\{K>0\}\) proves the point exactly because \(K\mid E=1\) has mass

\[
P(K=k\mid E=1)=\frac{e^{-\mu}\mu^k/k!}{1-e^{-\mu}},\qquad k\ge1.
\]

The source fact that automatic pitch-angle prescale is triggered by the previous 16-spin accumulation shows that at least some state variables can be feedback-generated, although the bundle does not establish that this prescale applies to the 3-D array. `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 342-351 and 515-531]`

### 0.2.2 Findings 3 and 4: count law and identification

The original distinction

\[
X\longrightarrow N^{\rm detected}\longrightarrow N^{\rm accepted}
\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow Y
\]

is retained. The repair is that no theorem now calls the current LP accumulator Poisson, negative-binomial, renewal, or Poisson-binomial merely because an event counter exists. Those are proposed model classes requiring the missing arithmetic and event-chain information.

The review overstates one point if read as invalidating the Poisson marginal-identification lemma itself. Under \(K_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\mu_j)\) and a documented zero-code inverse \(\{0,\ldots,d_j-1\}\),

\[
P(C_j=0)=e^{-\mu_j}\sum_{k=0}^{d_j-1}\frac{\mu_j^k}{k!}
\]

is strictly decreasing in \(\mu_j\); therefore the complete code law identifies \(\mu_j\). What failed was the application from identified \(\mu\) to LP \(U\). Section 5.3 now requires an explicit nuisance-eliminating anchor contrast, and Theorem 6.2 gives an exact energy-translation counterexample when plasma location nuisance is unrestricted.

### 0.2.3 Findings 5-8: dependence, calibration, selection, and mixing

Marginal means supply necessary restrictions on the joint observable law but are not sufficient unless the joint family is specified. The revised bounded-response set is therefore

\[
\Theta_I(Q)\subseteq\Theta_{\rm marg}(Q),
\]

with equality only under stated joint-law compatibility conditions.

For vector calibrated data, coordinatewise strictly increasing maps preserve the copula. Thus scalar quantile transport proves only scalar or marginal observational equivalence. The revised memo does not use that theorem to erase all vector information. It instead identifies the exact boundary: arbitrary joint randomized kernels can map every input law to the same output law; coordinatewise monotone maps cannot generally do so.

Selection is repaired at the level of the full observed law. For a common target density/mass \(q\) dominated by each preselection law \(p_u\), choose

\[
\pi_u(o)=r\frac{q(o)}{p_u(o)},\qquad
0<r\le\left\|q/p_u\right\|_\infty^{-1}
\]

for all compared \(u\). Then

\[
P_u(R=1,O\in do)=r q(do),\qquad P_u(R=0)=1-r,
\]

so the entire \((R,RO)\) experiment is identical. In a finite code space, a common atom such as the all-zero array yields the same construction with \(q\) a point mass.

A shared Gamma effect is not rejected. What is rejected is the inference that “compact mixing” is identifying. The compact singleton \(\{\delta_0\}\) makes every count zero. A valid positive shared-effect class must exclude that degeneration and either specify a finite-dimensional law or prove injectivity of the coded mixture operator.

### 0.2.4 Finding 9: corrected regularity statements

The condition

\[
\partial_u\mu\notin\operatorname{span}(D_\nu\mu)
\]

is required for nonzero regular first-order information, not for identification itself. The map \(u\mapsto1+u^3\) is locally one-to-one at zero despite zero derivative. The revised statement is explicitly about regular differentiable identification and efficient information.

A finite list of threshold equations can have infinitely many crossings. The plateau proposition therefore assumes finite crossings on the compact \(U\)-domain, justified for example by strict monotonicity or by analyticity on a neighborhood with no equation identically zero.

Uniform separation now uses the feasible graph

\[
\Gamma=\{(u,\nu):u\in\mathcal U,\ \nu\in\mathcal H_u\},
\]

which must be compact. Compactness of an ambient product is insufficient when \(u\mapsto\mathcal H_u\) has a nonclosed graph.

### 0.2.5 Findings 10 and 11: least-favorable order and discreteness

For fixed \((u,\nu_0)\), define

\[
p_{u,\nu_0}(o)=P_{u,\nu_0}\{T_u(O^*)\ge T_u(o)\}.
\]

This p-value is super-uniform even with atoms. Since

\[
p_u^{\rm LF}(o)=\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal H_u}p_{u,\nu}(o)
\ge p_{u,\nu_0}(o),
\]

\[
P_{u,\nu_0}\{p_u^{\rm LF}(O)\le\alpha\}
\le P_{u,\nu_0}\{p_{u,\nu_0}(O)\le\alpha\}\le\alpha.
\]

There is no mathematical defect to repair in that order. The terminology is repaired: this is exact finite-sample validity, not generally exact equality of size.

### 0.2.6 Findings 12 and 16: certificate direction

For a nonnegative residual \(\rho(u)=\inf_\nu r(u,\nu)\):

* a feasible witness with \(r(u,\tilde\nu)=0\) proves compatibility;
* a certified lower bound \(\underline\rho(u)>0\) proves incompatibility;
* \(\underline\rho(u)=0\) is inconclusive and must not exclude \(u\).

For law separation

\[
\Delta_\varepsilon=
\inf_{|u-v|\ge\varepsilon}\inf_{\nu,\xi}
 d\{P_{u,\nu}^O,P_{v,\xi}^O\},
\]

a certified \(\underline\Delta_\varepsilon>0\) proves uniform separation. A zero lower bound proves nothing. Nonidentification requires an exact equality witness or a certified upper bound equal to zero in exact arithmetic. These rules are now used in Sections 9, 13, and 14.

### 0.2.7 Findings 13 and 14: Monte Carlo and bootstrap

The review's optional-stopping example was independently reproduced by exact binomial recursion. At looks \(n=20,30,\ldots,3000\), stopping when an ordinary one-sided 99% Clopper-Pearson upper bound falls below the true \(p=0.05\) has crossing probability

\[
0.081525710238\ldots,
\]

not 0.01. The supplied script `audit/optional_stopping_audit.py` records the calculation. The revised primary procedure uses fixed budgets. Sequential use is permitted only with a summable look-wise failure budget or an anytime-valid confidence sequence.

The former GMM statement is retained only as the logical implication

\[
\sup_{u,\nu}P_{u,\nu}\{T_n(u)>c_{n,\alpha}(u)\}\le\alpha+o(1)
\Longrightarrow
\text{uniform coverage}.
\]

It is not evidence that an LP cluster bootstrap satisfies the premise. No such LP theorem is claimed.

### 0.2.8 Finding 15: calibration uncertainty

A frequentist response treatment now has only two admissible forms:

1. a deterministic envelope \(\mathcal E\) adopted as a maintained scientific restriction, in which case honesty is over laws whose true response lies in \(\mathcal E\); or
2. calibration data \(D_{\rm cal}\) and a region \(B_u(D_{\rm cal},O)\) satisfying
   \[
   \inf_{u,\nu}P_{u,\nu}\{\nu\in B_u\}\ge1-\gamma.
   \]

Engineering plausibility without either statement is sensitivity analysis, not propagated frequentist uncertainty. The current bundle supplies neither a validated envelope nor calibration sampling data, so present response-bounded LP inference is classified **D**.

# 1. Executive conclusion

The revised conclusion is narrower than the original and agrees with the referee's central objection.

**Current Lunar Prospector decision.** The case record does not establish a nontrivial honest frequentist confidence set for per-sweep spacecraft-relative potential \(U\), whether the declared observation is raw 3-D telemetry code \(C\) or archived calibrated flux \(Y\). No source-backed model in the bundle simultaneously supplies:

1. the exact observable-data experiment, including the opportunity frame, retention indicators, observed versus latent state, and whether recorded state is exogenous or count-generated;
2. an operational law for the accepted accumulator \(K\), including onboard arithmetic, terminal behavior, exposure, rejection, dead time, FIFO loss, masks, and state feedback;
3. a response/calibration nuisance set known or proved to contain the flight response;
4. a physical nuisance class that excludes exact translation confounding between \(U\) and an unknown plasma energy-location parameter; and
5. a global identification or partial-identification proof for that source-backed nuisance class.

Consequently, neither finite-sample, pointwise-asymptotic, nor uniformly asymptotic coverage has been established for the present LP application. A full-domain set would remain honest under a broad nonidentified class, but no justified nontrivial per-sweep set is available.

**Raw code route.** For ordinary nonterminal unprescaled codes, the deterministic compressor intervals are documented. Quantization itself does not preclude identification: under a proposed Poisson-marginal model, the zero-code probability is strictly monotone in the mean. But the current record does not define the joint law of the observable. The 3-D accumulator arithmetic, terminal-code rule, full state decoder for the 1999 version-5 record, accepted-event chain, effective exposure, response matrices, and calibration uncertainty are missing or unresolved. Moreover, known detector response would still not identify \(U\) if the physical nuisance class contains an unrestricted additive energy location. The current raw-code path is therefore **D - unresolved because named observation-law, calibration, and physical-anchor information is missing**. It is not promoted to A merely by assuming a separated profiled law.

**Calibrated-flux route.** The archived product is a state-dependent coarsening and transformation of compressed data. The bundle does not supply its complete deterministic map, conditional covariance law, response uncertainty, selection law, or calibration sampling distribution. Under a broad class permitting arbitrary cellwise response/background, arbitrary joint randomized calibration, or unrestricted outcome-dependent selection, explicit constructions make distinct \(U\)'s induce exactly the same full observable law, including retention. Such classes are **C - nontrivial honest inference impossible**. Coordinatewise monotone calibration alone is not enough to prove full-vector nonidentification because it preserves the copula; that original overstatement is withdrawn. The narrower intended LP calibrated-flux law is **D**, not C, because the case record does not define it well enough to prove either identification or equivalence.

**Positive mathematical boundary.** Observations of this form can support useful honest inference under assumptions weaker than ordinary independent Poisson, but only after the observation operator and physical anchor are supplied. The viable classes include:

* independent Poisson accepted counts with proved-exogenous design state, known compressor, and a response-normalized anchor contrast that is injective in \(U\);
* a specified finite-dimensional shared-effect model such as Gamma Cox with \(P(A>0)=1\), parameters tied across sweeps, known response, and an injective profiled coded law;
* negative-binomial models with dispersion bounded away from the all-zero boundary;
* known Poisson-binomial thinning, renewal/dead-time, or finite-state FIFO models whose accepted-count law is computable and whose response-folded map is identified;
* bounded response or loss classes, which generally yield a partially identified set and a computable honest outer confidence region rather than a point interval; and
* independent or otherwise concentration-controlled sweep/visit clusters with predeclared bounded observable features and source-backed expectation sets, allowing arbitrary dependence within each cluster.

These are conditional mathematical classes. None is established as the current LP flight law by the bundle.

**Primary conditional method.** Once a source-backed joint observation law and physical anchor exist, the recommended method is code-space composite-null test inversion. For each \(H_0:U=u\), evaluate interval probabilities

\[
P(C=c\mid u,\nu,D=d)
=\sum_{k\in I_d(c)}P(K=k, E=e,R=r\mid u,\nu,D=d)
\]

with the exact joint-state/selection factors appropriate to the declared observable. Use a predeclared statistic; profile nuisance inside the statistic; calibrate the generating-null nuisance by

\[
p_u^{\rm LF}(o)=\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal H_u}
 P_{u,\nu}\{T_u(O^*)\ge T_u(o)\};
\]

then invert \(\{u:p_u^{\rm LF}(O)>\alpha\}\). Theorem 11.1 gives finite-sample uniform coverage over the declared nuisance graph when the p-value is exact. A computational implementation may replace the nuisance supremum by a certified upper bound. Fixed-budget Monte Carlo, or sequential simulation with alpha spending/anytime-valid bounds, must account for Monte Carlo randomness. Ordinary repeatedly inspected Clopper-Pearson bounds are invalid.

**Best weak-law fallback.** With genuine replicated clusters, predeclared bounded features, and response-derived expectation sets, Theorem 11.6 constructs

\[
\mathcal C_\alpha^{\rm mom}
=\{u:\mathcal M_h(u)\cap B_n\ne\varnothing\},
\]

where \(B_n\) is a simultaneous finite-sample concentration box. This covers the true \(U\), and on the same event covers the entire moment-compatible set. It does not require within-sweep independence. It does require cluster independence or a proved replacement concentration inequality, and it is nontrivial only when the response/physical moment sets are separated. The current bundle does not supply the needed response expectation envelope, so this is a fallback design, not a current LP confidence procedure.

**Identification boundary.** The revised memo contains exact obstructions:

* arbitrary cellwise efficiency/background absorbs any physical loss-cone pattern;
* an unrestricted additive physical energy location absorbs \(U\) even with known detector response;
* arbitrary vector Cox mixing fixes the random intensity law across \(U\);
* a shared mixing class containing \(A=0\) has a common all-zero law, despite compactness;
* arbitrary outcome-dependent selection can match the complete \((R,RO)\) law, not merely the retained conditional law;
* arbitrary joint randomized calibration can erase all input-law information;
* contamination neighborhoods that overlap create partial or complete observational equivalence; and
* support plus the deterministic compressor admits a common all-zero observation law, forcing an honest nonrandomized set to be full-domain at that observation.

Point identification, finite-sample estimability, uniform distinguishability, and practical power are kept separate. A model can be point identified at every fixed nuisance value yet lack useful uniform inference when nuisance approaches an all-zero or weak-information boundary.

**PR 14.** The review strengthens, rather than reverses, the original negative disposition. PR 14 may retain its forward-model profile as a working sensitivity surface after correcting sentinels, permutation dependence, and failure semantics. It cannot be labeled an honest confidence set. Its present bootstrap is plug-in, omits the full selected pipeline and response uncertainty, does not take a generating-null nuisance supremum, uses an invalid no-bootstrap fallback, and has no pointwise or uniform coverage theorem. Adding simulation repetitions does not repair identification. The revised component table is in Section 16.

The final decision matrix distinguishes conditional mathematical classes from current LP evidence. The central disposition is:

* **A** only for explicitly specified, identified observation laws with a valid finite-sample or proved uniform-asymptotic test;
* **B** for bounded-response or moment classes with a computable honest outer set;
* **C** when an exact common-law or overlap obstruction makes nontrivial honest inference impossible; and
* **D** when a named source fact, calibration envelope, joint-law specification, or mathematical injectivity result is missing.

For the case record as delivered, the operational answer remains: **no nontrivial honest per-sweep confidence set is justified; the raw-code and intended calibrated-flux routes are unresolved D classes, while materially broader response/selection/support classes are C.**

# 2. Audited facts and unresolved source gaps

## 2.1 Instrument and archive facts

1. **Source fact—observational geometry and timing.** ER was spin-synchronous; the energy supply swept 32 times per spin, two sweeps were summed, fixed-angle samples from a half-spin were combined from 128 oversampled angular bins into 88 angular bins, and the retrace energy was discarded, leaving 15 energies. A real-time 3-D distribution is an \(88\times15\) array accumulated during one half-spin and read out once per 16 spins. `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 23–62; magerfmt.pdf, pp. 1–3]` The 1320 code bytes are ordered as 15 groups of 88 angles in ascending energy-step order. `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 551–560; magerfmt.pdf, p. 16]` The 3-D half-spin is the interval immediately preceding the computed accumulation end time \(T_{\rm Acc}\). `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 680–697; magerfmt.pdf, p. 20]`

2. **Inference—statistical unit.** The 15 rows are sequential components of one sweep, not 15 independent experiments. The angular cells share the same accumulation, response state, total-rate environment, and possible FIFO/dead-time losses. This agrees with the repository measurement contract. `[Bundle: current_branch/docs/architecture/er_measurement_contract.md, lines 308–320]`

3. **Source fact—lossy compressor.** All ER counter data are compressed to eight bits; the format is a four-bit exponent and four-bit hidden-bit mantissa; omitted mantissa bits are truncated, not rounded; and the tabulated decompressed value is the minimum accumulator value capable of producing that code. `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 326–328 and 964–971; magerfmt.pdf, pp. 10 and 27]` For \(c=16e+m\),

\[
L(c)=\begin{cases}
m,&e=0,\\
(16+m)2^{e-1},&e>0.
\end{cases}
\]

For an ordinary nonterminal unprescaled code, \(K\in\{L(c),\ldots,L(c+1)-1\}\). The table reaches code `FF`, whose documented lower endpoint is 507,904, but the document does not supply a state-specific terminal upper bound or an explicit overflow/saturation rule sufficient for a terminal-bin likelihood. `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 964–991; magerfmt.pdf, p. 27]`

4. **Source fact—scope of prescaling.** The document states that *pitch-angle data (not cuts)* may be prescaled, automatically or by command, in eight energy bands; the trigger is a prior pitch-angle sample above 524,287, and the affected values are divided by eight before compression. `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 342–351; magerfmt.pdf, p. 10]` The header description narrows this to the longer real-time pitch-angle accumulations and explicitly says high-time-resolution and cut data are not prescaled. `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 503–531; magerfmt.pdf, p. 15]`

5. **Inference—3-D prescale qualification.** The cited passages do not state that the \(88\times15\) 3-D array is prescaled by eight. A general observation operator should allow state-specific prescale where independently documented, but the 3-D code model must not apply pitch-angle prescale bits by analogy. This is a material qualification to the generic interval formula in the orientation memo.

6. **Source fact—diagnostic counters and losses.** The “Total Counts” header counter excludes masked-anode events and FIFO-overrun losses and is divided by 16 before compression. The “Rate Counter” further differs through pulse-height rejection, approximately 300 ns processing dead time, FIFO loss, and masks; it is prescaled in hardware and software. `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 532–550; magerfmt.pdf, pp. 15–16]` These counters are not exact sums of the 3-D cells and cannot be substituted as a row total without a proved state-specific relationship.

7. **Source fact—response gaps.** The full-instrument geometric factor is described approximately as \(0.07E_{\rm keV}\;\mathrm{cm^2\,sr\,keV}\), spread over 256 effective look directions, with significant anode-to-anode variation due to ADC differential nonlinearity and MCP gain. The anode efficiency table \(F\) is explicitly `TBS`; exact onboard phase-space arithmetic and rounding are referred to an “Appendix TBS.” `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 698–721; magerfmt.pdf, p. 20]` The introduction also points to a detailed instrument/operation description as `<TBS>`. `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 4–8; magerfmt.pdf, p. 1]`

8. **Source fact—Level-0 time and merge process.** The archived merged stream chooses between real-time and delayed records by matching timestamps and signal-to-noise, defaulting to real time in specified ties; delayed VCDU/ERT values are corrected by subtracting 3200 s; three selection/status flags are appended. `[Bundle: primary_sources/lvl0sis.txt, lines 492–523; lvl0sis.pdf, pp. 13–14]` A record is 472 bytes; MAG/ER occupies bytes 287–454; Earth Receive Time occupies bytes 457–462 and is the station receive time of the first sync bit, not the electron event time. `[Bundle: primary_sources/lvl0sis.txt, lines 524–626; lvl0sis.pdf, pp. 13–15]`

## 2.2 Calibrated-product facts

9. **Source fact—declared product.** The public 3-D product consists of 88 approximately equal solid-angle bins and 15 energy channels, with stated energy resolution \(\Delta E/E=25\%\); a spectrum is represented by 15 consecutive table records. `[Bundle: primary_sources/data-3deleflux-desc.txt, lines 19–48 and 149–166]` The rows are not simultaneous: the look azimuth changes as the energy sweep proceeds. `[Bundle: primary_sources/data-3deleflux-desc.txt, lines 41–48]`

10. **Source fact—processing chain.** The product description says processing decompresses raw counts, uses accumulation times depending on energy and solid angle, corrects for dead time using approximately \(0.3\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) and \(1/(1-RT)\), masks values when that factor exceeds 1.25, retains an approximately 10 counts/s cosmic-ray/noise background, and divides by \(0.02\;\mathrm{cm^2\,sr}\) and channel-center energy to obtain differential flux. `[Bundle: primary_sources/data-3deleflux-desc.txt, lines 80–114]` The low- and high-resolution product descriptions repeat this chain. `[Bundle: primary_sources/data-lowresflux-desc.txt, lines 69–98; primary_sources/data-highresflux-desc.txt, lines 69–100]`

11. **Source fact—scientific cautions.** Sunlight can contaminate the distribution; spacecraft charging shifts incoming energies and can bend low-energy trajectories; no correction for spacecraft-potential effects is applied to the archived product. `[Bundle: primary_sources/data-3deleflux-desc.txt, lines 256–281]`

12. **Source fact—reported uncertainty is incomplete.** The electron-product uncertainty description includes Poisson counting and digitization noise but explicitly excludes uncertainty in the absolute geometric factor, whose estimate depends on electrostatic optics, grids, and MCP efficiency. `[Bundle: primary_sources/data-electron-desc.txt, lines 104–112]` It does not provide the covariance or state-specific response coefficients needed for a likelihood.

13. **Source fact—missing-value sentinel.** The 1999 detached label defines `9.999E-99` as a missing constant, and a spectrum is 15 records. Its per-row timestamps are incremented from a spectrum timestamp using an approximate spin rate. `[Bundle: reference_data/1999_pr_day/3D990429.LBL, lines 27–40, 75–98, 117–120, and 156–165]`

## 2.3 Repository behavior and reproducible checks

14. **Source fact—current code is a structural extractor.** `decompress_er_counter` implements \(L(c)\), and the iterator assembles supported Digital Subcom versions into \(15\times88\) code arrays while rejecting incomplete, duplicate, mixed, out-of-order, or unsupported sequences. ## 2.4 Material conflicts and gaps

* **Energy response.** The calibrated-product description states \(\Delta E/E=25\%\), while PR 14 defaults to `de_over_e=0.5` and uniform quadrature over that width. `[Bundle: primary_sources/data-3deleflux-desc.txt, lines 19–26; pr14/src/losscone/quasi_likelihood.py, lines 30–46; pr14/src/losscone/response_folded.py, lines 39–63]` The bundle does not contain a state-specific response kernel that resolves this into a probability law. A width parameter is not a response matrix.

* **Geometric factors.** The telemetry document’s approximate full-instrument \(0.07E_{\rm keV}\) factor and the calibrated product’s \(0.02\;\mathrm{cm^2\,sr}\) conversion serve different levels of the chain and are not interchangeable. `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 698–704; primary_sources/data-3deleflux-desc.txt, lines 101–114; current_branch/docs/architecture/er_measurement_contract.md, lines 220–228]`

* **Prescale.** The documented prescale is for specified pitch-angle accumulations, not established for 3-D cells. A 3-D likelihood cannot use the expanded \(8L(c)\) interval without a 3-D state source.

* **Terminal code.** The lower endpoint is documented; the terminal upper endpoint, counter width, overflow behavior, and whether `FF` is saturation or simply the last representable logarithmic bin are not specified well enough for an exact terminal likelihood.

* **State/version.** The current parser’s version-1/2 support is adequate for the 1998 anchor but not a mission-wide decoder. Version 5 must be mapped from a commanding/interface or later format document before the 1999 raw record can enter inference.

* **Timing.** ERT and calibrated row timestamps do not by themselves identify each cell’s physical integration window. The header, spin period, phase, merge selection, and latency must be decoded and validated.

* **Response/loss.** Effective exposures, anode efficiencies, masks, background, dead-time, FIFO, pulse-height rejection, selection, and their uncertainty/covariance are not available at the cell/state level.

These are not minor nuisance omissions. Sections 5–6 prove that unconstrained versions of these unknowns can exactly absorb a change in \(U\).

# 3. Notation, probability space, estimand, and observation operators

## 3.1 Sampling frame and probability space

Let \((\Omega,\mathcal F,P)\) support incident particles, physical environment, instrument state and feedback, accepted-event dynamics, telemetry, ground processing, and selection. The sampling frame contains \(N_{\rm opp}\) potential 3-D opportunities in the declared analysis window. \(N_{\rm opp}\) may be fixed by design or random; whether it is observed is part of the experiment.

For opportunity \(i\), a cluster is one 3-D sweep or a prespecified visit containing several sweeps when dependence crosses sweep boundaries. Within a sweep, \(e\in\{1,\ldots,15\}\) indexes sequential energy rows, \(a\in\{1,\ldots,88\}\) indexes angular cells, \(j=(e,a)\), and \(d=1320\). The physical integration region is \(A_{ij}\subset\mathbb R_t\times\mathbb R_+\times\mathbb S^2\times\mathcal M\), with marks for anode, pulse height, trajectory class, and processor state.

Let \(R_i\in\{0,1\}\) denote retention in the analysis/product sample. A separate indicator may be introduced for each stage - telemetry availability, product generation, science-quality gate, and inferential regime gate - but the composition must be recorded. If only retained observations are available and the opportunity frame is unavailable, the statistical experiment is explicitly conditional on \(R_i=1\); it is not the full sampling-frame experiment.

## 3.2 Latent marked incident process and physical forward model

Let

\[
X_i=\sum_n\delta_{(T_{in},E_{in},\Omega_{in},M_{in})}
\]

be a marked incident-electron random measure at a declared physical reference surface, preferably the instrument aperture. It need not be Poisson. Its law belongs to a physical class \(\mathfrak X(U,\beta)\), where \(U\) is the target and \(\beta\) contains plasma distribution, mirror ratio, beam, background environment, and other physical nuisance.

A generic first-moment representation is

\[
\lambda_{ij}(U,\beta)=
\int_{A_{ij}}f_{U,\beta}(t,E,\omega,m)
\,dt\,dE\,d\omega\,dm,
\]

but no stochastic arrival law is implicit. The physical map may identify only \(U+\theta\), a plateau, or an equivalence class. This is tested before choosing an inferential objective.

## 3.3 State decomposition and endogeneity

Write the complete state as

\[
S_i=(D_i,E_i,S_i^{\rm lat}).
\]

Here:

* \(D_i\) is observed design state proved external or ancillary for the accepted-count model, such as an externally commanded setting when its exogeneity is documented;
* \(E_i\) is observed but potentially endogenous state or diagnostics, including any flag, prescale status, mask, dead-time status, FIFO status, or validity indicator generated from current or adjacent event activity; and
* \(S_i^{\rm lat}\) is unobserved state relevant to response or selection.

Ground-processing inputs are similarly split as \(Z_i=(Z_i^{\rm obs},Z_i^{\rm lat})\). Merely telemetering a state variable does not make it exogenous. For example, if \(K\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)\) and \(E=1\{K>0\}\), then \(K\mid E=1\) is zero-truncated Poisson. A likelihood may therefore condition on \(D_i\), but may condition on \(E_i\) while preserving the same count law only after a source-backed ancillarity argument. Otherwise it must model the joint law.

The instrument source states that automatic pitch-angle prescale can be triggered by the previous 16-spin accumulation and telemetered in the header. `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 342-351 and 515-531]` The same source does not establish that the 3-D array uses that prescale. The point is not to assert a particular 3-D feedback law, but to prohibit unproved conditioning on all decoded state.

## 3.4 Accepted accumulator and complete instrument law

Let \(\eta\) contain response and loss nuisance: exposure, energy/angular response, anode efficiency, geometric factor, background, pulse-height rejection, dead time, FIFO, masks, accumulation arithmetic, saturation, and selection.

The accepted integer vector immediately before telemetry compression is

\[
K_i=(K_{ij}:j=1,\ldots,d)\in\mathbb N_0^d.
\]

It is not the incident-arrival count unless the response/loss model proves that equality. An operational model supplies the joint kernel

\[
p_{u,\nu,i}(e,s^{\rm lat},k,r,z^{\rm lat}
 \mid d,z^{\rm obs}),
\qquad \nu=(\beta,\eta,\text{dependence nuisance}),
\tag{3.1}
\]

or a mathematically equivalent simulator. A conditional Poisson statement is valid only when the conditioning variables are those for which the kernel actually has that form.

## 3.5 Compression, calibrated flux, and the observable sigma-algebra

Conditional on complete compressor state \(s\), the code is deterministic:

\[
C_i=q_s(K_i)\in\{0,\ldots,255\}^d.
\]

For a scalar component, define

\[
I_s(c)=\{k\in\mathbb N_0:q_s(k)=c\}.
\]

For ordinary nonterminal unprescaled codes, \(I_s(c)=\{L(c),\ldots,L(c+1)-1\}\). Prescale arithmetic and terminal behavior are state-specific; when they are not documented, \(I_s(c)\) is a nuisance set rather than an invented interval.

Let the latent preselection calibrated product be

\[
Y_i^*=G(C_i,D_i,E_i,S_i^{\rm lat},Z_i^{\rm obs},Z_i^{\rm lat};\gamma),
\]

where \(\gamma\) denotes calibration parameters. The released value may be \(Y_i=Y_i^*\) when retained and a sentinel/missing value otherwise.

When the opportunity frame and retention indicators are available, the declared observable random element is

\[
O=\left(
N_{\rm opp},
\{D_i,E_i,Z_i^{\rm obs},R_i,R_iC_i,R_iY_i^*:1\le i\le N_{\rm opp}\}
\right),
\tag{3.2}
\]

with observable sigma-algebra

\[
\mathscr O=\sigma(O).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Products not present in a particular analysis are omitted from (3.2). Multiplication by \(R_i\) is notation for a marked missing value, not numerical multiplication of a code array. If \(N_{\rm opp}\) or rejected records are unavailable, define instead the conditional retained experiment

\[
O^{\rm ret}=\{D_i,E_i,Z_i^{\rm obs},C_i,Y_i^*:R_i=1\},
\qquad
P^{\rm ret}_{u,\nu}=P_{u,\nu}(\cdot\mid R=1),
\tag{3.4}
\]

and state coverage conditionally. The two experiments can have different identified sets.

The observable-law probability is the push-forward of (3.1). Let \(\dagger\) denote the observed missing mark when a code is not retained. For one opportunity,

\[
\begin{aligned}
&P_{u,\nu}(E=e,R=1,RC=c\mid D=d,Z^{\rm obs}=z)\\
&\quad=
\sum_{s^{\rm lat},z^{\rm lat}}
\sum_{k:q_{(d,e,s^{\rm lat})}(k)=c}
 p_{u,\nu}(e,s^{\rm lat},k,1,z^{\rm lat}\mid d,z),\\[3pt]
&P_{u,\nu}(E=e,R=0,RC=\dagger\mid D=d,Z^{\rm obs}=z)\\
&\quad=
\sum_{s^{\rm lat},z^{\rm lat}}
\sum_{k\in\mathbb N_0^d}
 p_{u,\nu}(e,s^{\rm lat},k,0,z^{\rm lat}\mid d,z).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Equation (3.5), not a likelihood conditional on hidden state, defines the observed mark. The unretained probability sums over the unobserved code rather than conditioning on a latent value. When \(E\) is proved exogenous, \(S^{\rm lat}\) is absent, and \(R=1\) by design, the first line reduces to the familiar conditional code probability.

No theorem treats \(L(C)\) or a rounded inverse of \(Y\) as observed \(K\).

## 3.6 Estimand

Fix the sign convention

\[
U=\Phi_{\rm surface}-\Phi_{\rm spacecraft}
\]

in volts, interpreted through the electron-energy convention of the physical forward model. The opposite barrier convention is \(-U\). The estimand is the value, identified set, or equivalence class of this relative shift.

If the physical map depends only on \(U+\theta\), where \(\theta\) is an unrestricted plasma energy-location nuisance, then \(U\) is not separately identified. If it depends only on the potential difference, the absolute pair is identified only up to common translation:

\[
(\Phi_{\rm surface},\Phi_{\rm spacecraft})
\sim
(\Phi_{\rm surface}+h,\Phi_{\rm spacecraft}+h).
\]

An independent spacecraft-potential or plasma-location anchor is required for stronger claims.

## 3.7 Model classes, nuisance graphs, and honest coverage

For a model class \(\mathcal M\), define the feasible nuisance graph

\[
\Gamma=\{(u,\nu):u\in\mathcal U,\ \nu\in\mathcal H_u\}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The observable-law set is

\[
\mathcal P_u^O=
\{\operatorname{Law}_{u,\nu}(O):(u,\nu)\in\Gamma\}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

A confidence set \(\mathcal C_\alpha(O)\subseteq\mathcal U\) is **finite-sample honest over \(\Gamma\)** if

\[
\inf_{(u,\nu)\in\Gamma}
P_{u,\nu}\{u\in\mathcal C_\alpha(O)\}\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{3.8}
\]

The probability in (3.8) includes randomized algorithms and Monte Carlo randomness. If inference is conditional on observed exogenous design \(D=d\), the guarantee is

\[
\inf_{(u,\nu)\in\Gamma_d}
P_{u,\nu}\{u\in\mathcal C_\alpha(O)\mid D=d\}
\ge1-\alpha
\tag{3.9}
\]

for every design value in the declared support. A conditional-on-retention guarantee replaces \(D=d\) by \((D=d,R=1)\) and does not cover the full preselection experiment.

Uniform asymptotic honesty for experiments \(n\) means

\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\inf_{(u,\nu)\in\Gamma_n}
P_{u,\nu}\{u\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(O_n)\}
\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Pointwise asymptotics fix \((u,\nu)\) before the limit and are weaker. A deterministic response envelope yields honesty only over the maintained class in which the true response lies in that envelope. A sensitivity range without (3.8)-(3.10) is not called a confidence set.

| Bounded response/moments | Nonlinear or mixed-integer feasibility with interval/global certificates | Coupled response/loss envelope; replicated clusters | Repeated-state code-threshold frequencies; anchor-product comparisons |
| Cluster moments/GMM | Feature computation and covariance/concentration calibration | Cluster design, source-backed expectation equations, hard feature bounds | Autocorrelation by visit; state-stratified moment stability; permutation tests |
| Robust neighborhoods | Minimax law optimization and certified radius | Nominal observation law and externally calibrated discrepancy radius | Held-out discrepancies, product cross-validation, state transfer error |
| Calibrated flux | Full covariance or push-forward simulation through ground map | Ground algorithm, sentinels, response covariance, selection | Raw/product association, sentinel rates, row-sum/covariance diagnostics |

Diagnostics can falsify an assumption or reveal weakness. Passing them does not prove that the nuisance class contains the true instrument law, does not prove identification, and does not establish coverage.

# 5. Identification analysis and proofs

## 5.1 Definitions

For feasible parameter pairs \((u,\nu),(v,\xi)\in\Gamma\), write

\[
(u,\nu)\equiv_O(v,\xi)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Law}_{u,\nu}(O)=
\operatorname{Law}_{v,\xi}(O).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Values \(u\) and \(v\) are observationally equivalent when

\[
\mathcal P_u^O\cap\mathcal P_v^O\ne\varnothing.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Given a complete observable law \(Q\), the identified set is

\[
\Theta_I(Q)=\{u\in\mathcal U:Q\in\mathcal P_u^O\}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Point identification means \(\Theta_I(Q)=\{u_0\}\) for every admissible true law \(Q\). Partial identification allows a proper nonsingleton set. Strong nonidentification occurs when a common law belongs to \(\mathcal P_u^O\) for every \(u\).

For a metric \(d\), usually total variation, define uniform separation at target distance \(\varepsilon>0\) by

\[
\Delta(\varepsilon)=
\inf_{\substack{(u,\nu),(v,\xi)\in\Gamma\\|u-v|\ge\varepsilon}}
 d\{P_{u,\nu}^O,P_{v,\xi}^O\}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Point identification requires only pairwise inequality of law sets. Uniform distinguishability requires \(\Delta(\varepsilon)>0\). Estimability from finitely many sweeps and practical confidence-set width are further questions.

## 5.2 Quantized-observation lemma

**Lemma 5.1 (push-forward through state, compression, and retention).**  
Fix observed exogenous design \(D=d\). Let complete state be \(s=(d,e,s^{\rm lat})\), let \(q_s:\mathbb N_0^d\to\{0,\ldots,255\}^d\) be deterministic, and let the joint kernel of endogenous state, latent state, accumulator, and retention be \(p_{u,\nu}(e,s^{\rm lat},k,r\mid d)\). Let \(\dagger\) be the observed missing-code mark. Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{u,\nu}(E=e,R=1,RC=c\mid D=d)
&=
\sum_{s^{\rm lat}}
\sum_{k:q_{(d,e,s^{\rm lat})}(k)=c}
 p_{u,\nu}(e,s^{\rm lat},k,1\mid d),\\
P_{u,\nu}(E=e,R=0,RC=\dagger\mid D=d)
&=
\sum_{s^{\rm lat}}
\sum_{k\in\mathbb N_0^d}
 p_{u,\nu}(e,s^{\rm lat},k,0\mid d).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Conditional on a fixed complete state \(s\) and retention,

\[
P(C=c\mid s)=\sum_{k\in I_s(c)}p_K(k\mid s),
\qquad
I_s(c)=\{k:q_s(k)=c\}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

If compression is componentwise, \(I_s(c)=\prod_j I_{s,j}(c_j)\), but (5.6) factors only if the accepted accumulators factor under the maintained law.

For a documented ordinary unprescaled nonterminal scalar code \(c<255\),

\[
I(c)=\{L(c),L(c)+1,\ldots,L(c+1)-1\}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

If a documented state first replaces the original accumulator by \(\lfloor K/d\rfloor\) and then applies the ordinary compressor, then

\[
I_d(c)=\{dL(c),dL(c)+1,\ldots,dL(c+1)-1\}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

For terminal code 255:

* if a finite counter maximum \(K_{\max}(s)\) and saturating rule are documented, \(I_s(255)=\{L(255),\ldots,K_{\max}(s)\}\);
* if all larger values are documented to map to 255, \(I_s(255)=\{L(255),L(255)+1,\ldots\}\); and
* if overflow, wrap, clipping, or terminal behavior is not documented, \(I_s(255)\) is not identified by the lower-endpoint table and must be treated as an unresolved state nuisance.

**Proof.** The event \(\{E=e,C=c,R=r\}\) is the disjoint union over latent states and integer vectors satisfying \(q_s(k)=c\), which gives (5.5) by countable additivity. Equation (5.6) is the conditional version. The source states that omitted bits are truncated and that the tabulated value is the minimum accumulator value producing the code; all values below the next lower endpoint therefore produce the same nonterminal code, proving (5.7). Under the stated integer division, \(q(\lfloor k/d\rfloor)=c\) iff

\[
L(c)\le\lfloor k/d\rfloor\le L(c+1)-1,
\]

which is equivalent to (5.8). A nonterminal table has no next endpoint after code 255 and therefore cannot determine the terminal inverse without a separate terminal rule. \(\square\)

**Instrument qualification.** The factor-eight prescale is documented for specified real-time pitch-angle accumulations, not for the 3-D array. `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 342-351 and 515-531]` Equation (5.8) may be used only after establishing applicability and exact integer arithmetic.

**Corollary 5.1.1 (lower endpoints do not identify \(K\)).**  
If an observed code has an inverse set containing at least two integers, two laws concentrated at different elements of that inverse set have the same code law and different means. If the terminal inverse is unbounded, the conditional mean within that code can be arbitrarily large or infinite without changing the code.

**Proof.** Both point masses have the same deterministic image. For an unbounded inverse, move the conditional mass to arbitrarily large integers or use a heavy-tailed conditional law. \(\square\)

## 5.3 What Poisson quantization identifies

**Proposition 5.2 (Poisson marginal mean from the zero code).**  
Assume that, conditional on proved-exogenous design \(D=d\), a scalar accepted accumulator has marginal law \(K_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\mu_j)\), \(0<\mu_j<\infty\), and the zero-code inverse is \(\{0,\ldots,d_j-1\}\) for a known integer \(d_j\ge1\). Then the complete marginal code law identifies \(\mu_j\), because

\[
P(C_j=0\mid D=d)
=F_{d_j-1}(\mu_j)
=e^{-\mu_j}\sum_{k=0}^{d_j-1}\frac{\mu_j^k}{k!}
\tag{5.9}
\]

is strictly decreasing from one to zero.

**Proof.** Differentiation gives

\[
F_m'(\mu)
=-e^{-\mu}\frac{\mu^m}{m!}<0,
\qquad \mu>0.
\]

The endpoint limits are immediate, so the map has a unique inverse. No cross-cell independence is needed for this marginal conclusion. \(\square\)

Quantization therefore need not erase the mean parameter. It can nevertheless make finite-sample information negligible when almost all mass lies in one code. It also does not identify the joint dependence law.

## 5.4 Identification under a concrete known-response baseline

A statement that “profiled law sets are disjoint” is a definition-level criterion, not a source-backed LP result. The following theorem gives a checkable sufficient structure.

**Theorem 5.3 (anchor-contrast identification under known response).**  
Fix an exogenous state \(D=d\) with positive design probability. Assume:

1. for cells in a nonempty anchor set \(J\), each \(K_j\) has a Poisson marginal with known zero-code inverse and mean \(\mu_j(u,\beta,d)>0\);
2. the compressor and all response factors entering these means are known;
3. there is a known map \(A:(0,\infty)^{|J|}\to\mathbb R\) and a one-to-one function \(\psi:\mathcal U\to\mathbb R\) such that
   \[
   A\{\mu_J(u,\beta,d)\}=\psi(u)
   \quad\text{for every feasible }(u,\beta);
   \tag{5.10}
   \]
4. the observable law includes the marginal code laws for the anchor cells.

Then \(U\) is point identified from the complete observable law. If \(\psi^{-1}\) is continuous and the estimated anchor means are consistent, the plug-in identifying functional is \(u=\psi^{-1}\{A(\mu_J)\}\); this statement alone is not a confidence theorem.

**Proof.** Proposition 5.2 identifies every \(\mu_j\), \(j\in J\), from the marginal code law. Hence the observable law identifies \(A(\mu_J)=\psi(u)\). Injectivity of \(\psi\) identifies \(u\). \(\square\)

**Corollary 5.3.1 (two-cell ratio anchor).**  
Suppose

\[
\mu_j(u,\rho,d)=\rho a_j f_j(u,d),\qquad
\mu_k(u,\rho,d)=\rho a_k f_k(u,d),
\]

where \(a_j,a_k>0\) and \(f_j,f_k>0\) are known and

\[
r(u)=\frac{a_jf_j(u,d)}{a_kf_k(u,d)}
\]

is one-to-one. Then \(A(\mu_j,\mu_k)=\mu_j/\mu_k=r(u)\) identifies \(u\) despite unknown common scale \(\rho\).

This corollary illustrates the kind of response tying and physical anchor required. The bundle does not establish such an LP contrast over the proposed physical nuisance class. In particular, unknown plasma energy location can defeat it by Theorem 6.2.

For an independent-Poisson baseline whose entire joint law is determined by \(\mu(u,\beta,d)\), disjoint profiled mean sets are equivalent to point identification. Without a mean-determined joint law, disjoint mean sets remain sufficient because equality of full laws implies equality of marginals, but intersecting mean sets need not prove equality of full laws.

## 5.5 Global uniform separation and regular local information

**Proposition 5.4 (compact feasible graph plus disjoint laws implies uniform separation).**  
Let

\[
\Gamma=\{(u,\nu):\nu\in\mathcal H_u\}
\]

be compact. Assume \((u,\nu)\mapsto P_{u,\nu}^O\) is continuous in total variation. If

\[
P_{u,\nu}^O\ne P_{v,\xi}^O
\quad\text{for every }(u,\nu),(v,\xi)\in\Gamma
\text{ with }|u-v|\ge\varepsilon,
\]

then \(\Delta(\varepsilon)>0\).

**Proof.** The feasible pair set

\[
\Gamma_\varepsilon=
\{((u,\nu),(v,\xi))\in\Gamma^2:|u-v|\ge\varepsilon\}
\]

is compact. Total variation distance is continuous and strictly positive on this set, so it attains a positive minimum. \(\square\)

The graph condition cannot be replaced by compactness of an ambient product when \(u\mapsto\mathcal H_u\) has a nonclosed graph.

**Proposition 5.5 (regular first-order identification condition).**  
Suppose the observable family is differentiable in quadratic mean at \((u_0,\nu_0)\), is locally parameterized through a differentiable finite-dimensional mean map \(\mu(u,\nu)\), and the map from \(\mu\) to the observable law is locally injective. If the Jacobian \(D_{(u,\nu)}\mu\) has full column rank at \((u_0,\nu_0)\), then the parameter pair is locally identified. If

\[
\partial_u\mu(u_0,\nu_0)
\in\operatorname{span}D_\nu\mu(u_0,\nu_0),
\tag{5.11}
\]

then the efficient first-order score for regular estimation of \(u\) is zero after nuisance projection and the regular efficient information for \(u\) is zero. Condition (5.11) is not necessary for identification in a nonregular model.

**Proof.** Full column rank makes a square subvector of \(\mu\) locally invertible by the inverse-function theorem; local injectivity of the law in \(\mu\) then gives local identification of \((u,\nu)\). Under differentiability in quadratic mean, score directions are the derivatives of the law along differentiable parameter paths. If (5.11) holds, the \(u\)-score lies in the closed nuisance tangent space, so its orthogonal projection onto the nuisance-orthocomplement is zero; therefore the efficient score and information vanish. The map \(\mu(u)=1+u^3\) at zero is one-to-one despite zero derivative, showing that first-order failure does not imply nonidentification. \(\square\)

## 5.6 Forward-model plateau obstruction with a finite-crossing condition

**Proposition 5.6 (finite-valued hard-grid forward maps identify only plateaus under finite crossings).**  
For fixed \(\beta\), suppose the finite-grid mean vector can be written

\[
\mu(U,\beta)=H_\beta\{r_1(U,\beta),\ldots,r_R(U,\beta)\},
\tag{5.12}
\]

where every \(r_q\) takes values in a finite set and changes value only when one of finitely many threshold or clipping-boundary equations is satisfied. Assume each such equation has finitely many solutions on compact \(\mathcal U\). Then \(\mu(U,\beta)\) is piecewise constant in \(U\). On every open interval between adjacent crossing points, all \(U\)'s induce the same law in any observation family whose law is determined by this mean and fixed nuisance.

**Proof.** The union of all crossing points is finite. On a connected component of its complement, every finite-valued regime label \(r_q\) is constant; (5.12) is therefore constant. \(\square\)

The finite-valued representation is substantive. A clipped quantity that still varies continuously inside an unclipped regime does not satisfy (5.12). Finite crossings follow, for example, when each threshold function is strictly monotone, or when it is real analytic on a neighborhood of \(\mathcal U\) and is not identically equal to the threshold. A finite number of equations alone is not enough: \(e^{-1/U^2}\sin(1/U)=0\) has infinitely many roots accumulating at zero.

The current finite pitch-grid hard mask can exhibit plateaus only to the extent that its \(U\)-dependence is genuinely finite-valued as in (5.12). The scientifically preferable repair is a continuous response integral with a verified nonzero identifying contrast; otherwise report any proved plateau as a partial identified set.

## 5.7 Code-space and calibrated-flux transformations

**Proposition 5.7 (known transformations and coarsening).**  
Let \(W\) denote all observed state carried into the ground map and let \(Y=g(C,W)\) be known.

1. If \(c\mapsto g(c,w)\) is one-to-one on the conditional support for every \(w\), then \((C,W)\) and \((Y,W)\) generate the same sigma-algebra and have the same identified set.
2. If the map is many-to-one, \((Y,W)\) is a coarsening and
   \[
   d_{\rm TV}(P_u^{Y,W},P_v^{Y,W})
   \le d_{\rm TV}(P_u^{C,W},P_v^{C,W}).
   \]
3. If the map depends on latent state or unknown calibration, the observable law is the corresponding mixture/push-forward; identification is governed by the allowed map class. Scalar monotonicity and coordinatewise vector monotonicity have different implications, as Section 6.4 proves.

**Proof.** A measurable bijection has a measurable inverse on the finite code support. Total variation contracts under any measurable push-forward. The third statement follows from the definition of the observable-law set. \(\square\)

For LP calibrated flux, the bundle does not establish a known bijective map from complete raw code/state records to \(Y\). Dead-time masking, missingness, rounding, response conversion, latent state, and product selection can remove information.

# 6. Impossibility and observational-equivalence proofs

## 6.1 Arbitrary cellwise response absorbs the physical pattern

Let \(f_j(u,\beta)\ge0\) be the ideal physical signal and suppose the accepted-count law is indexed through

\[
\mu_j=b_j+a_jf_j(u,\beta),
\tag{6.1}
\]

where \(a_j\) combines exposure, efficiency, geometric response, and accepted-event probability, and \(b_j\) is accepted background.

**Theorem 6.1 (cellwise response-absorption nonidentification).**  
If the nuisance class permits arbitrary \(a_j\ge0\) and \(b_j\ge0\) separately for every cell/state, then for every \(u_1\ne u_2\), every \(\beta_1,\beta_2\), and every \(m\in(0,\infty)^d\), there are response nuisances satisfying

\[
b_j^{(r)}+a_j^{(r)}f_j(u_r,\beta_r)=m_j,
\qquad r=1,2.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Any observation family whose complete joint law depends on these parameters only through the common vector \(m\), with the same state, compressor, and selection mechanism, gives exactly the same law of \(O\) at \(u_1\) and \(u_2\).

**Proof.** If \(f_j(u_r,\beta_r)>0\), take \(a_j^{(r)}=m_j/f_j(u_r,\beta_r)\) and \(b_j^{(r)}=0\). If it is zero, take \(a_j^{(r)}=0\) and \(b_j^{(r)}=m_j\). Equal complete accepted-count laws have equal deterministic compressed and calibrated push-forwards. \(\square\)

With zero background and positive signals, the simpler ratio adjustment

\[
a_j^{(2)}=a_j^{(1)}
\frac{f_j(u_1,\beta_1)}{f_j(u_2,\beta_2)}
\]

suffices. The construction is broken only by cross-cell/state restrictions such as known response ratios, low-dimensional tied response, anchor exposures, or sufficiently narrow coupled distortion bounds.

## 6.2 Known detector response does not remove physical translation confounding

**Theorem 6.2 (physical energy-location equivalence).**  
Suppose the ideal response-folded signal has the form

\[
f_j(u,\theta,\zeta)
=
\int R_j(E,\omega)
 g\{E-(u+\theta),\omega;\zeta\}
\,dE\,d\omega,
\tag{6.3}
\]

where \(R_j\) is completely known and \(\theta\) is an unrestricted physical energy-location nuisance. For any \(u_1,u_2\), choose

\[
\theta_2=\theta_1+u_1-u_2.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Then \(f_j(u_1,\theta_1,\zeta)=f_j(u_2,\theta_2,\zeta)\) for every cell. Any fixed instrument observation law driven by this signal is observationally equivalent at \(u_1\) and \(u_2\).

**Proof.** Equation (6.4) gives \(u_1+\theta_1=u_2+\theta_2\), so the integrands and all response-folded signals coincide. \(\square\)

Thus “known response” is not a sufficient identification statement. A near-minimal repair is an external anchor or a physical nuisance restriction that fixes or bounds the additive energy location independently of \(U\), together with an injective contrast as in Theorem 5.3.

## 6.3 Unrestricted intensity mixing

**Theorem 6.3 (arbitrary vector Cox mixing absorbs \(U\)).**  
Assume \(f_j(u,\beta)>0\) and random intensities

\[
\Lambda_j=A_jf_j(u,\beta).
\]

Conditional on \(\Lambda\), let the accepted-count/state law be a fixed kernel indexed by \(\Lambda\). If the joint law of the nonnegative vector \(A=(A_1,\ldots,A_d)\) is unrestricted, then \(U\) is not identified.

**Proof.** Fix any random vector \(Z\in(0,\infty)^d\). At each \(u\), set \(A_j^{(u)}=Z_j/f_j(u,\beta)\). Then \(\Lambda=Z\) almost surely for every \(u\), so the complete accepted and observed laws coincide. \(\square\)

**Proposition 6.4 (compact shared mixing is not sufficient).**  
A shared scalar mixing class can be compact and still destroy identification. If it contains \(H=\delta_0\), then under \(A=0\) all accepted counts and ordinary codes are zero for every \(U\). Hence the class has a common law.

**Proof.** The singleton \(\{\delta_0\}\) is compact. Under \(A=0\), every conditional Poisson intensity is zero and \(K=C=0\) almost surely. \(\square\)

A specified Gamma shared effect with shape bounded away from zero is not covered by this obstruction and remains a viable proposed model. A positive but otherwise nonparametric shared mixing class may preserve angular shape, but compactness and positivity alone do not prove injectivity of the finite coded-mixture operator. It is class D until such a theorem is supplied.

## 6.4 Unknown calibration: scalar transport, vector copulas, and arbitrary kernels

**Theorem 6.5 (scalar monotone-calibration equivalence).**  
Let scalar latent observations \(Z_r\) under \(u_r\), \(r=1,2\), have continuous strictly increasing CDFs \(F_r\). If the observed value is \(Y=h_r(Z_r)\) and the nuisance class permits an arbitrary strictly increasing calibration function, then for any \(h_1\),

\[
h_2=h_1\circ F_1^{-1}\circ F_2
\tag{6.5}
\]

makes \(h_2(Z_2)\) equal in law to \(h_1(Z_1)\).

**Proof.** \(F_2(Z_2)\) is uniform; applying \(F_1^{-1}\) gives law \(F_1\); applying \(h_1\) completes the transport. \(\square\)

The theorem applies to a scalar product or to each marginal when only marginal laws are used. It does not establish equality of a multivariate product under coordinatewise monotone transformations.

**Proposition 6.6 (coordinatewise monotone maps preserve the copula).**  
Let \(Z=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_d)\) have continuous marginals and copula \(C_Z\). For strictly increasing component maps \(h_j\), the vector \((h_1(Z_1),\ldots,h_d(Z_d))\) has the same copula. Consequently, two latent vector laws with different copulas cannot be made equal by coordinatewise strictly increasing calibration.

**Proof.** The marginal probability integral transforms satisfy

\[
F_{h_j(Z_j)}\{h_j(Z_j)\}=F_{Z_j}(Z_j).
\]

The joint law of these uniform variables, which is the copula, is unchanged. \(\square\)

**Counterexample 6.6.1.** Let \(X\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)\), \(Z^{(1)}=(X,X)\), and \(Z^{(2)}=(X,1-X)\). Both coordinates have identical uniform marginals, but one copula is comonotone and the other countermonotone. Coordinatewise increasing maps cannot equalize the joint laws.

**Proposition 6.7 (arbitrary joint randomized calibration destroys identification).**  
If the calibration nuisance is an arbitrary Markov kernel \(K_u(dy\mid z)\), then every input law can be mapped to a fixed output law \(Q\) by choosing \(K_u(dy\mid z)=Q(dy)\). Thus the full vector observable is nonidentified.

This proposition is mathematically exact but scientifically much broader than an unknown monotone calibration. The present LP calibrated product is not proved to belong to either a narrow coordinatewise-monotone class or an arbitrary-kernel class; its intended law is therefore D. Broad nonidentification is already established by response and selection when those are unrestricted.

## 6.5 Outcome-dependent selection with observable retention

Let \(O^*\) be the preselection observation for one opportunity with law \(P_u\), density or mass \(p_u\), and let \(R\) be generated with inclusion probability \(\pi_u(o)\). The full observed mark is \((R,RO^*)\).

**Theorem 6.8 (full selection equivalence).**  
Suppose a probability law \(Q\), with density/mass \(q\), is dominated by every compared \(P_u\), and \(q/p_u\) is essentially bounded. Choose a common

\[
0<r\le
\inf_u\left\|q/p_u\right\|_\infty^{-1}
\]

and set

\[
\pi_u(o)=r\frac{q(o)}{p_u(o)}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Then for every \(u\),

\[
P_u(R=1,O^*\in A)=rQ(A),
\qquad
P_u(R=0)=1-r.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Hence the complete law of \((R,RO^*)\), including retention probability, is identical across \(u\). With a fixed known number of independent opportunities, the complete retained-count experiment is also identical.

**Proof.** Integrating \(\pi_u p_u=rq\) over \(A\) gives the first equality; integrating over the whole space gives \(P(R=1)=r\), hence the second. \(\square\)

On a finite code space it is enough that the compared laws share one atom \(c_0\) with positive probability. Let \(Q=\delta_{c_0}\) and \(r\le\inf_uP_u(C=c_0)\). For Poisson, negative-binomial, and related count models with no forced positive minimum, the all-zero code is such an atom at every finite interior parameter. This repairs the former proof, which matched only the conditional retained law.

The obstruction is removed by a known external gate, a correctly modeled joint selection law, or valid inclusion-probability bounds. If the archive exposes only retained records, inference may target the conditional law (3.4), but its interpretation must remain conditional.

## 6.6 Distributionally robust neighborhoods

**Proposition 6.9 (overlap boundary for total-variation balls).**  
For \(B_{\rm TV}(P,\epsilon)=\{R:d_{\rm TV}(R,P)\le\epsilon\}\),

\[
B_{\rm TV}(P,\epsilon)\cap B_{\rm TV}(Q,\epsilon)\ne\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 d_{\rm TV}(P,Q)\le2\epsilon.
\tag{6.8}
\]

**Proof.** The forward implication is the triangle inequality. For the converse use \((P+Q)/2\), whose distance to each endpoint is half their mutual distance. \(\square\)

Thus a robust radius must be compared with the global separation of the profiled nominal law sets. Once neighborhoods overlap, point identification becomes partial; a common overlap for all \(U\) invokes the next theorem.

## 6.7 Broadest-class triviality

**Theorem 6.10 (common-law lower bound for honest confidence sets).**  
Let \(\mathcal U\subset\mathbb R\) have finite positive Lebesgue measure and suppose a law \(Q\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_u^O\) for every \(u\in\mathcal U\). Any confidence set satisfying (3.8) obeys

\[
E_Q|\mathcal C_\alpha(O)|
\ge(1-\alpha)|\mathcal U|.
\tag{6.9}
\]

On a grid of \(G\) values,

\[
E_Q\#\mathcal C_\alpha(O)\ge(1-\alpha)G.
\tag{6.10}
\]

If \(Q\) is a point mass and the procedure is nonrandomized, \(\mathcal C_\alpha(O)=\mathcal U\) at that observation for every \(\alpha<1\).

**Proof.** Honesty gives \(Q\{u\in\mathcal C_\alpha(O)\}\ge1-\alpha\) for each \(u\). Tonelli's theorem yields (6.9); summation yields (6.10). Under a point mass, each inclusion event has probability zero or one and must be one. \(\square\)

The support-plus-compressor-only class admits \(Q=\delta_{C\equiv0}\) for every \(U\), so nontrivial honest inference is impossible there.

## 6.8 Point identification without useful uniform inference

**Proposition 6.11 (negative-binomial all-zero boundary).**  
For a negative-binomial law with mean \(\mu>0\) and shape \(r>0\),

\[
P(K=0)=\left(\frac{r}{r+\mu}\right)^r.
\tag{6.11}
\]

As \(r\downarrow0\), \(P(K=0)\to1\), uniformly for \(\mu\) in any compact subset of \((0,\infty)\). Hence the count and compressed laws converge in total variation to the all-zero law. If the nuisance class permits \(r\downarrow0\), separated mean values are not uniformly distinguishable.

**Proof.** Write

\[
\log P(K=0)=-r\log(1+\mu/r).
\]

The right side tends uniformly to zero on compact \(\mu\)-sets because \(r\log(1+M/r)\to0\). Total variation from \(\delta_0\) equals \(1-P(K=0)\). Push-forward cannot increase total variation. \(\square\)

Thus fixed-interior point identification does not imply uniform separation over a class approaching \(r=0\). This proposition does **not** prove that every finite-sample honest confidence set is trivial when \(r>0\): exact least-favourable inversion may remain valid and nontrivial for informative observations. It proves that no procedure can have a uniform shrinking-width or uniformly powerful guarantee over that boundary-approaching class. If the model actually includes \(r=0\), or another nuisance value producing the common all-zero law, Theorem 6.10 gives the stronger C-class obstruction. A positive lower bound on shape or an equivalent information restriction is required for uniform usefulness.

## 6.9 Exchangeability is not identification

Finite exchangeability only imposes permutation symmetry. Infinite exchangeability yields a mixture representation under the relevant state space, but an unrestricted mixing measure can include the all-zero law and arbitrary response-equivalent components. Neither form identifies \(U\) without conditional mean, response, state, and physical-anchor restrictions. Exchangeability can support resampling only after the actual exchangeable units and the target conditional experiment are established.

# 7. Interval-code likelihood derivations

## 7.1 State-specific endpoints

For an observed scalar code \(c\), let \(a_s(c)=\inf I_s(c)\) and \(b_s(c)=\sup I_s(c)\), allowing \(b_s(c)=\infty\) only when the terminal rule is documented. For ordinary nonterminal unprescaled data,

\[
a(c)=L(c),\qquad b(c)=L(c+1)-1.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For a documented divide-by-\(d\) prescale followed by truncating compression,

\[
a_d(c)=dL(c),\qquad b_d(c)=dL(c+1)-1.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Every formula below uses the exact inverse set, not the decompressed lower endpoint alone. The endpoint representation \([a_s(c),b_s(c)]\) is valid only when \(I_s(c)\) is one contiguous integer interval. If a documented wrap, lookup table, or other nonmonotone operation makes

\[
I_s(c)=\bigcup_{q=1}^{Q(c,s)}[a_{sq}(c),b_{sq}(c)]\cap\mathbb N_0
\]

a disjoint finite union, the code probability is the sum of the corresponding interval probabilities, evaluated with a log-sum-exp. An undocumented terminal rule is not replaced by an upper tail, wrap interval, or saturation rule.

## 7.2 Independent Poisson accepted counts

Let \(K\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)\). Define \(F(k;\mu)=P_\mu(K\le k)\), with \(F(-1;\mu)=0\), and \(S(k;\mu)=P_\mu(K>k)\). For a finite interval \([a,b]\),

\[
\pi_{a,b}(\mu)
=P_\mu(a\le K\le b)
=F(b;\mu)-F(a-1;\mu)
=S(a-1;\mu)-S(b;\mu).
\tag{7.3}
\]

For a terminal upper tail,

\[
\pi_{a,\infty}(\mu)=S(a-1;\mu).
\tag{7.4}
\]

For ordinary code zero, \(a=b=0\) and \(\log\pi=-\mu\). For a prescaled zero code, \(\pi=F(d-1;\mu)\).

### Stable log differences

Let `log1mexp(x)` for \(x\le0\) be evaluated as

\[
\operatorname{log1mexp}(x)=
\begin{cases}
\log\{-\operatorname{expm1}(x)\},&-\log2<x\le0,\\
\log1p\{-e^x\},&x\le-\log2.
\end{cases}
\tag{7.5}
\]

If \(a=0\), use \(\log F(b;\mu)\). If \(b=\infty\), use \(\log S(a-1;\mu)\). Otherwise either exact identity is available:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\pi_{a,b}
&=\log F(b;\mu)
 +\operatorname{log1mexp}\{\log F(a-1;\mu)-\log F(b;\mu)\},\\
&=\log S(a-1;\mu)
 +\operatorname{log1mexp}\{\log S(b;\mu)-\log S(a-1;\mu)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.6}
\]

Use the lower-tail representation when the library returns accurate non-saturated log-CDFs and the upper-tail representation when the interval lies in the upper tail; if both are finite, their agreement is a numerical check. Never compute `cdf(b)-cdf(a-1)` in ordinary floating point when the terms are close.

A useful derivative is

\[
\frac{d}{d\mu}\pi_{a,b}(\mu)
=p_{\mu}(a-1)-p_{\mu}(b),
\tag{7.7}
\]

with \(p_\mu(-1)=0\); for \(b=\infty\), the derivative is \(p_\mu(a-1)\). Gradients of the log likelihood follow by division by \(\pi\), using log-domain ratios.

If \(D_i\) is proved exogenous, no endogenous state is conditioned on without modeling it, and cells and clusters are independent under the maintained law, then

\[
\ell(u,\nu;C,D)=
\sum_i\sum_j
\log\pi_{a_{D_i,j}(C_{ij}),b_{D_i,j}(C_{ij})}
\{\mu_{ij}(u,\nu,D_i)\}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

If a recorded state \(E_i\) is count-generated, the contribution must instead be the joint probability \(P(E_i=e_i,C_i=c_i,R_i=r_i\mid D_i)\) from (5.5). Factorization is invalid when accepted counts share a random effect, FIFO, normalization, state feedback, selection, or other dependence.

## 7.3 Negative-binomial interval likelihood

Use the mean/shape parameterization

\[
P(K=k)=
\frac{\Gamma(k+r)}{\Gamma(r)k!}
\left(\frac{r}{r+\mu}\right)^r
\left(\frac{\mu}{r+\mu}\right)^k,
\qquad r>0.
\tag{7.9}
\]

Then \(E K=\mu\) and \(\operatorname{Var}K=\mu+\mu^2/r\). The code probability is

\[
\pi^{\rm NB}_{a,b}(\mu,r)
=F_{\rm NB}(b;\mu,r)-F_{\rm NB}(a-1;\mu,r),
\tag{7.10}
\]

or the corresponding survival difference, evaluated by the same log-difference rule. For a uniformly informative A-class model, take \(r\) known or profile it over \([r_{\min},r_{\max}]\) with \(r_{\min}>0\), use a proved-exogenous conditioning design or a joint state model, and impose the anchor/injectivity condition of Section 5. If the exact family instead allows every \(r>0\) with no positive lower bound, finite-sample least-favourable inversion can still be honest when the coded family is point identified and the nuisance supremum is computable, but Proposition 6.11 rules out uniform separation and uniform width/power guarantees. Including an actual all-zero boundary value gives the C-class obstruction of Theorem 6.10.

## 7.4 Shared-gamma Cox likelihood

A scientifically useful dependent alternative is

\[
A_i\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(r,r),\qquad
K_{ij}\mid A_i,D_i\ \overset{\rm ind}{\sim}\ 
\operatorname{Pois}\{A_i\mu_{ij}(u,\nu,D_i)\},
\tag{7.11}
\]

where the gamma uses shape \(r\), rate \(r\), so \(EA_i=1\). Marginally each cell is negative-binomial, while cells in a sweep are positively correlated through \(A_i\). The exact code-rectangle probability is

\[
P(C_i=c_i\mid u,\nu,D_i)
=\int_0^\infty
\prod_{j=1}^d
\pi_{a_{ij},b_{ij}}\{a\mu_{ij}(u,\nu,D_i)\}
\frac{r^r}{\Gamma(r)}a^{r-1}e^{-ra}\,da.
\tag{7.12}
\]

With \(x=ra\),

\[
P(C_i=c_i)=
\frac1{\Gamma(r)}
\int_0^\infty x^{r-1}e^{-x}
\prod_j\pi_{a_{ij},b_{ij}}
\left(\frac{x}{r}\mu_{ij}\right)dx.
\tag{7.13}
\]

This is a one-dimensional generalized Gauss–Laguerre integral. Adaptive quadrature in log scale is also feasible. The integrand should be accumulated as

\[
(r-1)\log x-x-\log\Gamma(r)+\sum_j\log\pi_{a_{ij},b_{ij}}(x\mu_{ij}/r),
\]

with log-sum-exp across nodes. Quadrature error must be bounded and propagated conservatively into the test statistic; Section 13 gives a certification rule.

A lognormal shared effect is similar but uses Gauss–Hermite or adaptive integration. An unrestricted vector effect is class C by Theorem 6.3. A merely compact shared-mixing class is not automatically valid: it must exclude \(A=0\) degeneracy and satisfy a coded-mixture injectivity result, as Proposition 6.4 explains.

## 7.5 Poisson-binomial and heterogeneous thinning

If \(K=\sum_{\ell=1}^N B_\ell\) with independent \(B_\ell\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_\ell)\), the probability generating polynomial is

\[
G(z)=\prod_{\ell=1}^N\{(1-p_\ell)+p_\ell z\},
\tag{7.14}
\]

and \(P(K=k)=[z^k]G(z)\). The interval-code probability is \(\sum_{k=a}^b[z^k]G(z)\). Coefficients can be computed by dynamic programming in \(O(N^2)\) for the full PMF, truncated DP in \(O(Nb)\), divide-and-conquer FFT, or a certified saddlepoint approximation with explicit error control. This model is appropriate only when the finite opportunities and efficiencies have physical meaning. Arbitrary unknown \(p_\ell\) or cellwise thinning recreates Theorem 6.1.

## 7.6 Nonparalyzable dead time and renewal models

For a Poisson attempted-arrival process of rate \(\lambda\) observed for exposure \(T\), with deterministic nonparalyzable dead time \(\tau\) after each accepted event, the time of the \(k\)th accepted event is

\[
T_k=G_k+(k-1)\tau,
\quad G_k\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(k,\lambda).
\tag{7.15}
\]

Therefore, for \(T-(k-1)\tau\ge0\),

\[
P(N_T\ge k)=P\{G_k\le T-(k-1)\tau\},
\tag{7.16}
\]

and it is zero otherwise. The accepted-count PMF is

\[
P(N_T=k)=P(N_T\ge k)-P(N_T\ge k+1).
\tag{7.17}
\]

Summing (7.17) over the code interval gives an exact scalar likelihood. The formula does not cover paralyzable dead time, event-dependent service, shared FIFO, or anode masks; those require their own state process.

For a finite-capacity FIFO with a finite-state continuous-time Markov chain, augment the generator with a counting reward. If \(Q(z)\) is the tilted generator whose accepted-transition rates are multiplied by \(z\), then the probability generating function over exposure \(T\) is

\[
G_T(z)=\pi_0^\top e^{Q(z)T}\mathbf 1.
\tag{7.18}
\]

Coefficients, and hence code intervals, can be obtained by FFT inversion on a circle with truncation/error certification. This becomes multivariate if accepted events are routed to cells. The model is A only when arrival, service, routing, mask, and initial-state rules are specified or bounded tightly enough for identification.

## 7.7 Conditional Poisson and the missing latent total

If exact counts in one row satisfy independent \(K_a\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\rho p_a(u,\nu))\), then conditional on \(N=\sum_aK_a\),

\[
(K_1,\ldots,K_A)\mid N
\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}\{N,p(u,\nu)\},
\tag{7.19}
\]

which eliminates \(\rho\). For interval codes, however, exact conditioning is

\[
P(C=c\mid N,u,\nu)
=\sum_{k\in I(c):\sum_a k_a=N}
\frac{N!}{\prod_a k_a!}\prod_a p_a(u,\nu)^{k_a}.
\tag{7.20}
\]

If \(N\) is unobserved, it must be summed with its model probability. Normalizing decompressed lower endpoints or calibrated flux by its row sum is not (7.19) or (7.20). The separately telemetered Total Counter is not the exact 3-D row total: it covers all energies including retrace and has its own masks, FIFO loss, and factor-16 prescale. `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 532–550]`

## 7.8 General dependent law

For any full multivariate accepted-count law conditional on complete state,

\[
P(C_i=c_i\mid S_i=s_i)=
P\{K_i\in I_{s_i}(c_i)\mid s_i\}
=\sum_{k\in I_{s_i}(c_i)}p_{K,i}(k\mid s_i).
\tag{7.21}
\]

When state is latent or endogenous, observable probabilities are obtained by the joint summation in (5.5), not by inserting the realized state into (7.21) and retaining an unconditional count law.

A composite likelihood formed by multiplying marginal interval probabilities may estimate marginal parameters under conditions, but it is not the full likelihood. A likelihood-ratio cutoff from the composite criterion is invalid unless its distribution is calibrated under the complete cluster law. Cluster sandwich covariance can support asymptotic estimating-equation inference; it cannot restore identification or make a misspecified composite likelihood exact.

# 8. Weaker stochastic models and their tractability

## 8.1 Conditional Poisson and shared Cox effects

A common sweep-level amplitude \(A_i\) can represent density or exposure variation while the response-normalized shape carries \(U\). The specified Gamma model in (7.11)-(7.13) is weaker than independent Poisson because it allows overdispersion and positive within-sweep dependence. It is viable only when:

1. the conditioning design is proved exogenous or the joint state law is included;
2. \(P(A_i>0)=1\) and the mixing parameters are restricted away from the all-zero boundary;
3. one common or explicitly low-rank effect is tied across cells and sweeps;
4. the coded mixture operator and physical forward map satisfy an anchor/injectivity condition; and
5. selection and response uncertainty are included.

An arbitrary vector effect is class C by Theorem 6.3. A merely compact scalar mixing-law class is not enough by Proposition 6.4. A positive nonparametric mixing distribution can be considered only after proving injectivity for the finite coded observation; absent that result it is D.

If an exact latent row total were observed, independent Poisson counts with mean \(\rho p_a(u)\), \(\sum_a p_a=1\), could condition on that total and eliminate \(\rho\). Componentwise compressed codes do not reveal it. A valid coded conditional likelihood must use (7.20) and sum over compatible latent totals, or use an independently proved exact total. Normalized flux is not a substitute.

## 8.2 Explicit overdispersion families

Negative-binomial, Poisson-lognormal, and finite mixtures can model extra-Poisson variation. A uniformly informative positive class requires a scientifically tied finite-dimensional family, a closed feasible graph or an analytic tail reduction, lower tail/information bounds excluding all-zero degeneration, the full dependence induced by mixing, and the response/selection conditions of Section 3. A family name or a compact set of arbitrary distributions does not supply these facts.

Zero inflation illustrates the boundary. If its mass at zero can approach one but remains below one, every fixed interior parameter may be identifiable and exact least-favourable inversion may remain finite-sample honest, while uniform separation and uniform width/power fail. If mass one is included, the common all-zero law invokes Theorem 6.10 and the class is C.

## 8.3 Heterogeneous efficiency and Poisson-binomial models

A Poisson-binomial model is appropriate only when a finite set of potential events and event-specific acceptance probabilities has physical meaning. It is not automatically weaker than Poisson: it assumes finite support and conditional Bernoulli structure. Known efficiencies give a computable interval law; coupled bounds yield partial identification. Arbitrary efficiencies reproduce response absorption. If attempted events are Poisson and independently thinned with fixed probability, the accepted process is Poisson; shared detector state, dead time, or FIFO loss invalidates that reduction.

## 8.4 Renewal, refractory, and finite-capacity models

Known nonparalyzable dead time yields renewal-tail probabilities (7.15)-(7.17). A finite-state FIFO can be handled by Markov-additive generating functions (7.18). These are complete alternatives to Poisson only when attempted arrivals, exposure, queue service, routing, masks, initial state, accumulator arithmetic, and any observed feedback flags are modeled jointly. The source confirms a four-event FIFO and event-processing losses in the Total Counter path, but it does not define a state-resolved 3-D cell law. `[Bundle: primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 532-550]`

Unrestricted cell-specific loss is algebraically equivalent to arbitrary response and is C. Validated coupled loss bounds give B.

## 8.5 Clustered and finite-range-dependent sweeps

Let \(O_i\) be the complete sweep/visit observation defined by (3.2), not a vector of 1320 independent cells. A weaker model can allow arbitrary within-cluster dependence while assuming clusters are independent conditional on observed design, or satisfy a predeclared finite-range/mixing condition with a proved concentration theorem.

The estimand must be common across pooled clusters, follow a stated regression \(U_i=g(W_i;\theta)\), or be locally constant in a prespecified window. Otherwise pooling changes the target to an unspecified mixture. Cluster boundaries cannot be chosen after inspecting residual autocorrelation without including that selection in calibration.

## 8.6 Martingale/counting-process restrictions

If an accepted counting path \(N_j(t)\) is observed and has compensator \(\Lambda_j(t;u,\nu)\), then

\[
M_j(t)=N_j(t)-\Lambda_j(t;u,\nu)
\]

can support exponential-supermartingale or Freedman/Bernstein tests under bounded jumps and predictable variation. With only terminal codes, one can reject a candidate only if every latent path/count vector compatible with the code and state violates the bound. This requires finite terminal upper bounds or a tail envelope. The bundle supplies neither the accepted path nor a certified compensator, so the current route is D.

## 8.7 Exchangeability

Finite exchangeability supplies symmetry, not a response law. Infinite exchangeability supplies a mixture representation only for genuinely comparable infinite units; the mixing law remains nuisance and may include the common all-zero mechanism. Time order, energy order, angular geometry, and state prevent treating the 1320 cells as exchangeable without a source-backed transformation. Exchangeability alone is C.

## 8.8 Estimating-equation and conditional-mean models

Let \(h_i(O_i)\) be observed cluster features and suppose a source-backed restriction gives

\[
E_{u_0,\nu_0}\{m_i(u_0,\nu_0)\mid D_i\}=0.
\tag{8.1}
\]

No full count distribution is required. A singleton profiled zero set gives point identification; a nonsingleton set gives partial identification. Bounded features and independent clusters yield the finite-sample construction in Theorem 11.6. Unbounded GMM or empirical likelihood requires a separate uniform triangular-array theorem. Sandwich covariance addresses variance of valid moments only; it does not repair response absorption, translation confounding, or selection.

## 8.9 Moment inequalities

Quantization, terminal uncertainty, and bounded response naturally yield inequalities. These can support an honest B-class outer region when observable feature expectations can be estimated with a simultaneous finite-sample bound. They do not become sharp for the full joint law merely because marginal means are Poisson. Section 9 distinguishes the exact identified set, the marginally necessary outer set, and its finite-sample confidence outer approximation.

# 9. Partial-identification construction

## 9.1 Exact identified set versus a marginal outer set

Fix a proved-exogenous state/design stratum and let \(Q\) denote the complete observable law in that stratum. Suppose each accepted accumulator has a Poisson marginal and the zero-code inverse is known. Proposition 5.2 identifies the vector of marginal means

\[
\mu(Q)=\{\mu_j(Q):j=1,\ldots,d\}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Let the coupled response/calibration envelope be \(\mathcal E\), and write

\[
\mu_j(u,\beta,\eta)=
 b_j(\eta)+a_j(\eta)f_j(u,\beta),
\qquad (\beta,\eta)\in\mathcal B\times\mathcal E.
\tag{9.2}
\]

The exact identified set remains

\[
\Theta_I(Q)=
\{u:\exists\nu\in\mathcal H_u\text{ such that }P_{u,\nu}^O=Q\}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Define the marginal-mean compatibility set

\[
\Theta_{\rm marg}(Q)=
\left\{u:\exists(\beta,\eta)\in\mathcal B\times\mathcal E
\text{ such that }
\mu_j(Q)=\mu_j(u,\beta,\eta)
\ \forall j
\right\}.
\tag{9.4}
\]

**Proposition 9.1 (bounded-response marginal outer set and sharpness conditions).**  
Under the stated Poisson marginal and response-envelope assumptions,

\[
\Theta_I(Q)\subseteq\Theta_{\rm marg}(Q).
\tag{9.5}
\]

Equality holds under either of the following additional conditions:

1. the complete joint observable law is uniquely determined by the mean vector and the remaining nuisance in (9.4), as under independent Poisson accepted counts with fixed state, compressor, and selection; or
2. whenever two feasible parameter values have the same marginal mean vector, the nuisance class permits the same complete joint accepted-count/state/selection law at both values.

Without such a condition, (9.4) is a necessary outer identified set and need not be sharp.

**Proof.** If \(u\in\Theta_I(Q)\), some feasible nuisance reproduces the complete law \(Q\). Its marginal zero-code probabilities equal those of \(Q\), so Proposition 5.2 forces its marginal means to equal \(\mu(Q)\), proving (9.5). Under condition 1, equality of means and the remaining specified nuisance reproduces the complete law. Under condition 2, the true complete dependence/state law can be reused at the candidate value whenever means agree. Conversely, without either condition, equal means need not imply equal joint laws. For example, two Poisson(1) marginals can be independent or perfectly shared through \(K_1=K_2=Z\), \(Z\sim\operatorname{Pois}(1)\); their joint code laws differ. \(\square\)

Thus the former claim of unconditional sharpness under “Poisson marginals” is withdrawn.

## 9.2 Concrete bounded-response forms

A common envelope is

\[
a_j\in[a_j^-,a_j^+],\qquad
b_j\in[b_j^-,b_j^+],
\tag{9.6}
\]

possibly coupled by smoothness, shared scales, calibration covariance ellipsoids, or response-kernel constraints. Independent cellwise boxes can be scientifically too broad and may leave Theorem 6.1 feasible. Tied constraints are preferred, for example

\[
a_j=a_j^0\exp\{x_j^\top\eta\},
\qquad \eta^\top V^{-1}\eta\le c,
\tag{9.7}
\]

or a stochastic response matrix whose rows lie in a jointly calibrated convex set.

For fixed \((u,\beta)\), componentwise boxes imply the necessary interval

\[
b_j^-+a_j^-f_j(u,\beta)
\le\mu_j(Q)\le
b_j^++a_j^+f_j(u,\beta),
\tag{9.8}
\]

when all quantities are nonnegative. Coupled envelopes require solving the joint feasibility problem rather than checking (9.8) separately.

A deterministic envelope is a maintained assumption: frequentist honesty is over models whose true response lies in it. If the envelope is estimated from calibration data, its estimation error must be included through Section 11.2 or joint inversion.

## 9.3 Finite-sample outer confidence set from repeated zero-code indicators

Suppose \(n\) independent clusters share the same \(U\), response state, and scalar marginal mean \(\mu_j\) for each cell \(j\). Dependence between cells within a cluster is arbitrary. Let

\[
Z_{ij}=1\{C_{ij}=0\},
\qquad X_j=\sum_{i=1}^n Z_{ij}.
\]

For each \(j\), construct an exact two-sided binomial interval

\[
[p_j^L,p_j^U]
\]

with failure probability \(\alpha_j\), and choose \(\sum_j\alpha_j\le\alpha\). Because \(p_j=F_{d_j-1}(\mu_j)\) is decreasing, define

\[
\mu_j^L=F_{d_j-1}^{-1}(p_j^U),
\qquad
\mu_j^U=F_{d_j-1}^{-1}(p_j^L),
\tag{9.9}
\]

with endpoint conventions \(F^{-1}(1)=0\), \(F^{-1}(0)=\infty\).

Let

\[
\widehat\Theta_{\rm marg,\alpha}=
\left\{u:\exists(\beta,\eta)
\text{ such that }
\mu_j(u,\beta,\eta)\in[\mu_j^L,\mu_j^U]
\ \forall j
\right\}.
\tag{9.10}
\]

**Proposition 9.2 (finite-sample confidence outer set for the marginal identified set).**  
Under independent clusters and constant state/means,

\[
P\{\Theta_{\rm marg}(Q)\subseteq
\widehat\Theta_{\rm marg,\alpha}\}\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{9.11}
\]

In particular, the true \(U\) is covered with probability at least \(1-\alpha\) whenever the maintained response envelope and marginal model are correct.

**Proof.** Each binomial interval covers \(p_j\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha_j\). The union bound gives simultaneous coverage of all \(p_j\), hence all \(\mu_j\), with probability at least \(1-\alpha\). On that event every population-compatible nuisance remains feasible in (9.10), so the entire set (9.4) is retained. \(\square\)

This construction uses only zero-code indicators and does not require terminal-code rules. It does require identical marginal probabilities across the repeated stratum and a valid treatment of selection. With varying design, use cluster-specific bounded features and Theorem 11.6 instead of pretending that a single \(p_j\) applies.

## 9.4 General distribution-free interval moments

For any finite inverse interval \(I_s(c)=[L_s(c),U_s(c)]\), the state label \(s\) may be conditioned on only when it is observed and proved exogenous for the count law, or when the conditional feature expectation is derived from a specified joint state/count law. With latent or endogenous state, define the expectation restrictions by integrating over its declared law or by taking the least-favorable union over admissible state laws; substituting one decoded-state endpoint is invalid.

\[
L_s(C_{ij})\le K_{ij}\le U_s(C_{ij}).
\tag{9.12}
\]

Taking expectations gives

\[
E L_s(C_{ij})\le E K_{ij}\le E U_s(C_{ij}),
\tag{9.13}
\]

provided the upper endpoint is finite. This is distribution-free but may be weak. Threshold indicators are often better:

\[
1\{C_{ij}\le c\}
=1\{K_{ij}\le U_s(c)\}
\tag{9.14}
\]

for documented monotone nonterminal coding. A response/count model can bound the expectation of (9.14) without reconstructing \(K\).

Let \(h_r(O_i)\in[a_{ir},b_{ir}]\) be such features and let \(\mathcal M_h(u)\) be the set of possible average expectations under response, state, selection, and stochastic nuisance. The finite-sample outer set is the intersection construction of Theorem 11.6.

## 9.5 Correct numerical certification of feasibility

Let a nonnegative residual summarize compatibility, for example

\[
\rho(u)=
\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal H_u}
\max_j
\frac{\operatorname{dist}\{\mu_j(u,\nu),[\mu_j^L,\mu_j^U]\}}{s_j}.
\tag{9.15}
\]

The valid logic is:

* a feasible witness \(\tilde\nu\) with residual zero proves that \(u\) is compatible with the outer constraints;
* a certified global lower bound \(\underline\rho(u)>0\) proves incompatibility and permits exclusion;
* a certified lower bound equal to zero is inconclusive; and
* optimizer failure, unresolved branching, or a positive feasible residual from a local search cannot justify exclusion.

Therefore a conservative numerical outer set is

\[
\widehat\Theta^{\rm num}=
\{u:\text{no strictly positive global lower bound on }\rho(u)
\text{ has been certified}\}.
\tag{9.16}
\]

This may retain infeasible values, which widens the set but preserves outer coverage.

## 9.6 Targets of uncertainty statements

Three targets must not be conflated:

1. a **confidence set for the true parameter** satisfies \(P\{u_0\in C\}\ge1-\alpha\);
2. a **confidence region covering the identified set** satisfies \(P\{\Theta_I(Q)\subseteq C\}\ge1-\alpha\); and
3. a **confidence outer region for a moment/marginal outer set** covers \(\Theta_{\rm marg}(Q)\) or the moment-compatible set, which may be larger than \(\Theta_I(Q)\).

Propositions 9.2 and Theorem 11.6 provide the third and therefore also the first under the maintained model. They do not prove that the reported set is sharp for the full observable law.

# 10. Derived optimization and testing objectives

## 10.1 Exact code-space likelihood route

For a specified joint observable law on the experiment (3.2), including any endogenous state and retention mechanism, define

\[
\widehat\theta=(\widehat u,\widehat\nu)
\in\arg\max_{u\in\mathcal U,\nu\in\mathcal H_u}\ell(u,\nu;O),
\tag{10.1}
\]

where \(\ell\) is the observable-data log likelihood obtained from (5.5), using interval probabilities, latent-state integration, retention, and the full dependence law. A likelihood conditional on an endogenous flag while retaining an unconditional Poisson count law is not admissible. This is an estimator, not a confidence set. For testing \(H_0:u=u_0\), use the generalized likelihood-ratio statistic

\[
T_{u_0}(O)=
2\left[
\sup_{u,\nu}\ell(u,\nu;O)
-
\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal H_{u_0}}\ell(u_0,\nu;O)
\right].
\tag{10.2}
\]

Nuisance is handled in the null distribution by least-favorable or Berger–Boos calibration, not merely by profiling the observed likelihood. Critical values are obtained from the exact null experiment as in Section 11. No Wilks or \(\chi^2_1\) cutoff is asserted at boundaries, under weak identification, with discrete codes, or with selected data.

A score or discrepancy statistic can replace (10.2) if its null law is easier to bound. Coverage comes from the test’s size, not from the choice of optimizer.

## 10.2 Cluster estimating equations and GMM

Let \(m_i(u,\nu)\in\mathbb R^R\) be cluster moments and \(\bar m_n=n^{-1}\sum_i m_i\). An estimator minimizes

\[
Q_n(u,\nu)=n\bar m_n(u,\nu)^\top W_n(u,\nu)\bar m_n(u,\nu).
\tag{10.3}
\]

A weak-identification-robust test of \(u=u_0\) is

\[
T_n(u_0)=
\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal H_{u_0}}
 n\bar m_n(u_0,\nu)^\top
\widehat\Sigma_n(u_0,\nu)^+
\bar m_n(u_0,\nu),
\tag{10.4}
\]

where \(+\) is the Moore–Penrose inverse on the estimable moment subspace. The covariance must be estimated across sweep/visit clusters, including all 15 rows and 88 cells in one cluster feature. Critical values require either the finite-sample concentration route of Theorem 11.6, a least-favorable Gaussian bound, subsampling with a proved uniform theorem, or a cluster bootstrap whose uniform validity conditions are proved for the actual triangular array. No such LP bootstrap theorem is established here. Optimizer curvature in (10.3) is not a critical value.

## 10.3 Empirical likelihood

For independent clusters and a fixed \((u,\nu)\), empirical likelihood maximizes \(\prod_i(np_i)\) subject to

\[
p_i\ge0,\quad\sum_i p_i=1,\quad\sum_i p_i m_i(u,\nu)=0.
\tag{10.5}
\]

When feasible, the dual statistic is

\[
R_n(u,\nu)=2\sum_{i=1}^n\log\{1+\lambda^\top m_i(u,\nu)\},
\tag{10.6}
\]

where \(\lambda\) solves \(\sum_i m_i/(1+\lambda^\top m_i)=0\). Profile with \(\inf_\nu R_n(u,\nu)\). The familiar chi-square limit requires interiority of zero in the convex hull, fixed moment dimension, nonsingular covariance on the moment span, smoothness, and a uniform CLT. Quantization causes no special problem when moments are defined on observed codes, but sparse/degenerate code features can violate convex-hull and covariance conditions. Finite-sample concentration is safer near those boundaries.

## 10.4 Moment inequalities and partial identification

Let \(g_r(O;u,\nu)\) be oriented so that \(E g_r\le0\) under the null. A generic statistic is

\[
T_n(u)=
\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal H_u}
\max_{r\le R}
\left[
\frac{\sqrt n\,\bar g_r(u,\nu)}{\widehat\sigma_r(u,\nu)}
\right]_+.
\tag{10.7}
\]

Moment selection and studentization can improve power but complicate uniform calibration. The finite-sample alternative is to form simultaneous confidence intervals for \(Eg_r\) and retain \(u\) whenever some nuisance makes every inequality compatible. This directly yields an outer confidence region; it does not select an arbitrary optimizer within a flat identified set. Under unspecified joint dependence, the target is the moment/marginal outer set of Section 9, not a sharp full-law identified set.

## 10.5 Distributionally robust tests

For a nominal law family \(P_{u,\nu}^0\) and a law neighborhood \(\mathcal N_{u,\nu}\), a level-\(\alpha\) robust test solves

\[
\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal H_u}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_{u,\nu}}E_P\varphi_u(O)
\le\alpha,
\tag{10.8}
\]

while maximizing worst-case power against separated alternatives. Equivalently, for a statistic \(T_u\), use the robust p-value

\[
p_u^{\rm rob}(o)=
\sup_{\nu}\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_{u,\nu}}
P\{T_u(O^*)\ge T_u(o)\}.
\tag{10.9}
\]

Inversion is honest over the neighborhood. If null and alternative neighborhoods overlap, Proposition 6.9 limits power and may force full sets. A robustness radius must come from calibration/validation evidence; choosing it after seeing interval width invalidates the guarantee.

## 10.6 Calibrated-flux quasi-likelihood

A calibrated-flux Gaussian criterion is a genuine likelihood only if the declared observable experiment includes the relevant opportunity/retention information and, conditional only on proved-exogenous design (or under a correct joint selection/state model),

\[
Y_i\sim N\{m_i(u,\nu),\Sigma_i(u,\nu)\}
\tag{10.10}
\]

with the specified covariance and support treatment. If only \(E(Y_i)=m_i(u,\nu)\) is maintained, minimizing a weighted quadratic criterion is an M-estimator. Its asymptotic covariance is the cluster sandwich

\[
A^{-1}BA^{-\top},
\quad
A=E\left[-\frac{\partial\psi_i}{\partial\theta^\top}\right],
\quad B=E(\psi_i\psi_i^\top),
\tag{10.11}
\]

where \(\psi_i\) is the sweep/visit score. \(B\) must include shared row normalization, sequential rows, calibration correlations, and random effects. A single sweep cannot estimate a repeated-cluster sandwich without additional structure. A valid sandwich theorem is at most pointwise unless its moment, covariance, rank, selection, and nuisance conditions hold uniformly.

If rows are normalized to proportions, their covariance is singular and all free coordinates are correlated. A valid criterion uses a full-rank contrast and its full covariance, a generalized inverse on the simplex tangent, or a correctly transformed logistic-coordinate density. Dropping an arbitrary cell and assigning diagonal variances is generally order-dependent.

## 10.7 Invariance for compositional angular data

Let \(p,q\in\mathbb R^A\) be compositions with \(\mathbf1^\top p=\mathbf1^\top q=1\), let \(r=p-q\), and let \(\Sigma\) be positive definite on the tangent space

\[
\mathcal S=\{x:\mathbf1^\top x=0\},
\]

with null space spanned by \(\mathbf1\).

**Theorem 10.1 (contrast invariance).**  
Let \(H\) be any \((A-1)\times A\) contrast matrix whose restriction to \(\mathcal S\) is one-to-one. Then

\[
(Hr)^\top(H\Sigma H^\top)^{-1}(Hr)
=r^\top\Sigma^+r.
\tag{10.12}
\]

Thus the full-covariance quadratic statistic is invariant to which cell is dropped and to any nonsingular coordinate basis on the simplex tangent.

**Proof.** Let \(B\) be an \(A\times(A-1)\) matrix with orthonormal columns spanning \(\mathcal S\). Write \(r=Bx\) and \(\Sigma=B\Omega B^\top\), where \(\Omega\) is positive definite. Since \(H\) is injective on \(\mathcal S\), \(HB\) is square and nonsingular. Then

\[
(Hr)^\top(H\Sigma H^\top)^{-1}(Hr)
=x^\top(HB)^\top\{HB\Omega(HB)^\top\}^{-1}HBx
=x^\top\Omega^{-1}x.
\]

Also \(\Sigma^+=B\Omega^{-1}B^\top\), so \(r^\top\Sigma^+r=x^\top\Omega^{-1}x\). \(\square\)

**Counterexample to diagonal dropped-cell weighting.** Let \(A=3\), residual \(r=(1,-1,0)\), and assign coordinate variances \((1,4,9)\). Dropping cell 3 gives \(1^2/1+(-1)^2/4=1.25\); dropping cell 2 gives \(1^2/1+0^2/9=1\). The criterion changes under relabeling. PR 14’s `cols[:-1]` plus diagonal model-dependent variances has this defect. `[Bundle: pr14/src/losscone/quasi_likelihood.py, lines 61–100]`

Log-ratio coordinates can be used, but invariance then requires the correct Jacobian/density or delta-method full covariance. Merely changing which denominator cell defines an additive log ratio does not preserve a diagonal working criterion.

## 10.8 When log-MSE is legitimate

**Proposition 10.2 (log-MSE likelihood characterization and approximation regime).**  
Assume \(Y_j>0\) are conditionally independent and form a homoskedastic log-location family

\[
p(y_j\mid\theta)=\frac1{y_j}
 f\{\log y_j-\log\mu_j(\theta)\},
\qquad y_j>0,
\tag{10.13}
\]

with a common density \(f\) not depending on \(j\) or \(\theta\). Then

\[
Q(\theta)=\sum_j\{\log Y_j-\log\mu_j(\theta)\}^2
\tag{10.14}
\]

is, up to a positive multiplicative constant and terms independent of \(\theta\), the negative log likelihood for all data and parameter values if and only if

\[
f(x)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}
\exp\left(-\frac{x^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)
\tag{10.15}
\]

for some common known \(\sigma^2>0\). Thus the corresponding observation law is independent lognormal noise with median \(\mu_j(\theta)\). If \(\mu_j\) denotes the arithmetic mean under that law, the log center is \(\log\mu_j-\sigma^2/2\), not \(\log\mu_j\). Unequal or correlated log errors require inverse-covariance weighting and the corresponding determinant term when covariance depends on parameters.

For \(Y=\mu(1+x)\) with \(|x|\le\rho<1\),

\[
\{\log(Y/\mu)\}^2=x^2-x^3+R_4(x),
\tag{10.16}
\]

where

\[
|R_4(x)|\le C_\rho x^4,
\quad
C_\rho=\frac14+
\frac{2(1+\rho/2)}{3(1-\rho)^3}
+\frac{\rho^2}{9(1-\rho)^6}.
\tag{10.17}
\]

Thus log-MSE approximates relative-error least squares only for uniformly small relative errors, away from zero.

**Proof.** Under (10.13), the only \(\theta\)-dependent term in the negative log likelihood is \(-\sum_j\log f(x_j)\), with \(x_j=\log Y_j-\log\mu_j(\theta)\). If it equals \(c\sum_jx_j^2\) plus a parameter-independent term for every residual vector, the one-observation case gives \(-\log f(x)=cx^2+c_0\); normalization yields (10.15), with \(c=1/(2\sigma^2)>0\). The converse is immediate. The arithmetic-mean correction is \(E e^{N(m,\sigma^2)}=e^{m+\sigma^2/2}\).

For the approximation, Taylor's theorem gives

\[
\log(1+x)=x-\frac{x^2}{2}+R_3,
\qquad |R_3|\le\frac{|x|^3}{3(1-\rho)^3}.
\]

Squaring and using \(|x-x^2/2|\le |x|(1+\rho/2)\) and \(|x|^6\le\rho^2x^4\) gives (10.16)-(10.17). \(\square\)

For large Poisson \(K\), the delta method gives approximately \(\operatorname{Var}(\log K)\approx1/\mu\) and bias approximately \(-1/(2\mu)\), so unweighted log-MSE is not the Poisson likelihood even before quantization. Code zeros make \(\log L(C)\) undefined; adding an arbitrary epsilon changes the objective. Wide code intervals, the positive missing sentinel, dead-time masking, and shared normalization further violate the approximation. The legacy Halekas log criterion is therefore a shape heuristic, not a likelihood or calibrated goodness-of-fit statistic. `[Bundle: current_branch/src/losscone/chi2.py, lines 35–55]`

# 11. Confidence-set algorithms and coverage proofs

## 11.1 Least-favorable test inversion

For each candidate \(u\), choose a measurable statistic \(T_u(O)\), larger values being less compatible with \(H_0:U=u\). The statistic may profile nuisance internally, for example the likelihood ratio (10.2). For each generating-null nuisance \(\nu\in\mathcal H_u\), define

\[
p_{u,\nu}(o)=
P_{u,\nu}\{T_u(O^*)\ge T_u(o)\},
\tag{11.1}
\]

where \(O^*\) is drawn from the exact observable experiment (3.2), including opportunity counts, state, retention, quantization, ground processing, and any randomized analysis. Define

\[
p_u^{\rm LF}(o)=
\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal H_u}p_{u,\nu}(o).
\tag{11.2}
\]

Assume this supremum has a measurable version. A sufficient condition is that \(\mathcal H_u\) is a compact metric space and \((o,\nu)\mapsto p_{u,\nu}(o)\) is jointly measurable and upper semicontinuous in \(\nu\). Without such a condition, the test must use a measurable upper envelope of the supremum; a nonmeasurable pointwise supremum is not an inferential procedure.

**Theorem 11.1 (finite-sample honest least-favorable inversion).**  
The test \(1\{p_u^{\rm LF}(O)\le\alpha\}\) has size at most \(\alpha\) for every \((u,\nu)\in\Gamma\). Therefore

\[
\mathcal C_\alpha^{\rm LF}(O)=
\{u:p_u^{\rm LF}(O)>\alpha\}
\tag{11.3}
\]

satisfies finite-sample uniform coverage (3.8).

**Proof.** Fix the true \(\nu_0\). For \(T=T_u(O)\), the nonrandomized upper-tail p-value \(p_{u,\nu_0}(O)=P(T^*\ge T\mid T)\) is super-uniform. A direct proof follows by letting \(G(t)=P(T\ge t)\): the set \(\{t:G(t)\le x\}\) is an upper set and has probability at most \(x\). Atoms make the inequality conservative. Since \(p_u^{\rm LF}\ge p_{u,\nu_0}\),

\[
P_{u,\nu_0}(p_u^{\rm LF}\le\alpha)
\le P_{u,\nu_0}(p_{u,\nu_0}\le\alpha)
\le\alpha.
\]

Noncoverage of the true \(u\) is exactly rejection. \(\square\)

This theorem is finite-sample valid at boundaries, under quantization, weak identification, and disconnected confidence sets. It does not require Wilks. It is conditional on the declared nuisance class containing the true observation law.

## 11.2 Berger-Boos reduction and calibration-data uncertainty

Let \(B_u(O,D_{\rm cal})\subseteq\mathcal H_u\) satisfy

\[
\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal H_u}
P_{u,\nu}\{\nu\in B_u(O,D_{\rm cal})\}
\ge1-\gamma.
\tag{11.4}
\]

The calibration data may be independent or jointly modeled with the science data; (11.4), not independence by itself, is the requirement. Define

\[
p_u^{\rm BB}=
\min\left\{1,
\sup_{\nu\in B_u}p_{u,\nu}+\gamma
\right\}.
\tag{11.5}
\]

**Theorem 11.2 (Berger-Boos validity).**  
Rejecting when \(p_u^{\rm BB}\le\alpha\) has size at most \(\alpha\).

**Proof.** If the true nuisance lies in \(B_u\) and \(p_u^{\rm BB}\le\alpha\), then its simple-null p-value is at most \(\alpha-\gamma\). Hence

\[
P(p_u^{\rm BB}\le\alpha)
\le P(p_{u,\nu_0}\le\alpha-\gamma)
 +P(\nu_0\notin B_u)
\le\alpha.
\]

\(\square\)

A plug-in response estimate, an engineering “reasonable range,” or a profile curvature region is not a Berger-Boos set unless (11.4) is proved. A fixed deterministic response envelope is instead a maintained restriction defining \(\mathcal H_u\).

## 11.3 Exact-rank simulation under a simple null

For a fully specified \((u,\nu)\), simulate a fixed number \(B\) of complete datasets from the same experiment and apply the identical measurable analysis algorithm, including quantization, calibration, missingness, selection, nuisance refitting inside the statistic, and optimizer failure rules. The algorithm is either deterministic or is augmented by independent identically distributed algorithmic randomizers for the observed and every simulated dataset, all independent of the complete datasets. Define

\[
\widehat p_{u,\nu}^{\rm rank}
=
\frac{1+\sum_{b=1}^B
1\{T_u(O^{*(b)})\ge T_u(O)\}}
{B+1}.
\tag{11.6}
\]

**Theorem 11.3 (finite-sample valid Monte Carlo rank test).**  
Under the fully specified simple null, rejecting when \(\widehat p_{u,\nu}^{\rm rank}\le\alpha\) has size at most \(\alpha\).

**Proof.** The observed and simulated complete datasets are exchangeable. Applying the same measurable algorithm preserves exchangeability. The observed statistic's upper rank is uniform without ties and conservative with the greater-than-or-equal convention. \(\square\)

This is not a parametric-bootstrap theorem for an estimated nuisance: simulations from \(P_{u,\widehat\nu(O)}\) are not exchangeable with \(O\) conditional on \(\widehat\nu\) in general.

## 11.4 Fixed-budget certified Monte Carlo for a composite null

A computational least-favorable p-value can be upper-certified without optional-stopping error.

Fix candidate \(u\). Before simulation, choose a finite cover

\[
\mathcal H_u\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^J B_j
\]

with representatives \(\nu_j\) and deterministic certified bounds

\[
\sup_{\nu\in B_j}
 d_{\rm TV}(P_{u,\nu}^O,P_{u,\nu_j}^O)
\le\epsilon_j.
\tag{11.7}
\]

For each \(j\), use a fixed predeclared simulation count \(B_j^{\rm MC}\). Conditional on observed \(o\), the simulations within representative \(j\) are independent draws from \(P_{u,\nu_j}^O\) and use the same deterministic analysis map, or independent identically distributed algorithmic randomizers as in Section 11.3. Let \(X_j\) be the number of simulated statistics at least \(T_u(o)\), and let \(U_j(X_j;\delta_j)\) be a one-sided exact binomial upper confidence bound satisfying

\[
P_{\rm MC}\{p_{u,\nu_j}(o)\le U_j\mid o\}
\ge1-\delta_j.
\]

Set \(\delta_{\rm MC}=\sum_j\delta_j\) and

\[
\bar p_u(o)=
\min\left\{1,
\max_j\{U_j+\epsilon_j\}
\right\},
\qquad
p_u^{\rm cert}(o)=
\min\{1,\bar p_u(o)+\delta_{\rm MC}\}.
\tag{11.8}
\]

**Theorem 11.4 (finite-sample valid fixed-budget nuisance certificate).**  
Under (11.7), fixed simulation budgets, the conditional simulation scheme just stated, and exact binomial bounds, the test rejecting \(H_0:u\) when \(p_u^{\rm cert}\le\alpha\) has size at most \(\alpha\), where probability includes data and Monte Carlo randomness.

**Proof.** Conditional on \(o\), a union bound gives an event \(E_o\) of Monte Carlo probability at least \(1-\delta_{\rm MC}\) on which all representative tail probabilities are below their \(U_j\)'s. Total variation then gives

\[
p_u^{\rm LF}(o)
\le\max_j\{p_{u,\nu_j}(o)+\epsilon_j\}
\le\bar p_u(o).
\]

On \(E_o\), rejection implies \(p_u^{\rm LF}(o)\le\alpha-\delta_{\rm MC}\). Therefore, under the true nuisance,

\[
P(\text{reject})
\le P\{p_u^{\rm LF}(O)\le\alpha-\delta_{\rm MC}\}
 +P(E_O^c)
\le\alpha.
\]

\(\square\)

Berger-Boos adds \(\gamma\) in the same way: reject only when \(\bar p_u+\delta_{\rm MC}+\gamma\le\alpha\).

## 11.5 Valid sequential simulation

Ordinary fixed-look binomial intervals may not be repeatedly inspected at the same nominal confidence level. Two valid alternatives are:

1. an anytime-valid binomial confidence sequence; or
2. a predeclared sequence of looks \(\ell\) with bounds having failure probabilities \(\delta_{j\ell}\) satisfying
   \[
   \sum_{j,\ell}\delta_{j\ell}\le\delta_{\rm MC}.
   \tag{11.9}
   \]

At look \(\ell\), use a fixed-sample exact upper bound at level \(1-\delta_{j\ell}\). The union bound makes all bounds simultaneous over every look and nuisance box, so stopping based on them is valid.

**Proposition 11.5 (alpha-spending sequential validity).**  
Replacing the fixed bounds in Theorem 11.4 by the simultaneous family described in (11.9) preserves its size bound under arbitrary data-dependent stopping and refinement along the predeclared countable tree.

**Proof.** With probability at least \(1-\delta_{\rm MC}\), every bound at every permitted look is valid. On that event the proof of Theorem 11.4 applies at the stopping time. \(\square\)

The review's repeated 99% Clopper-Pearson example has a false-certificate probability \(0.081525710238\ldots\), not 0.01. This is reproduced in the delivery audit.

## 11.6 Finite-sample bounded-feature moment outer set

Let \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) be independent clusters conditional on observed designs \(D_i=d_i\); they need not be identically distributed. Choose predeclared features

\[
h_{ir}(O_i)\in[a_{ir},b_{ir}],
\qquad r=1,\ldots,R.
\]

Define the observed average \(\bar h_r=n^{-1}\sum_i h_{ir}\). For candidate \(u\), let the set of possible average expectations be

\[
\mathcal M_h(u)=
\left\{
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
E_{u,\nu}\{h_i(O_i)\mid D_i=d_i\}:
\nu\in\mathcal H_u
\right\}.
\tag{11.10}
\]

Set

\[
\delta_r=
\sqrt{
\frac{\log(2R/\alpha)}{2n^2}
\sum_{i=1}^n(b_{ir}-a_{ir})^2
},
\qquad
B_n=\prod_r[\bar h_r-\delta_r,\bar h_r+\delta_r],
\tag{11.11}
\]

and

\[
\mathcal C_\alpha^{\rm mom}
=
\{u:\mathcal M_h(u)\cap B_n\ne\varnothing\}.
\tag{11.12}
\]

**Theorem 11.6 (finite-sample honest moment-box outer set).**  
Under conditional cluster independence and the hard bounds above,

\[
P_{u_0,\nu_0}\{u_0\in\mathcal C_\alpha^{\rm mom}
\mid D_1=d_1,\ldots,D_n=d_n\}
\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{11.13}
\]

On the same event, every \(u\) whose expectation set contains the true average expectation is retained. Thus the procedure covers the entire moment-compatible set with probability at least \(1-\alpha\).

**Proof.** Hoeffding's inequality for independent, nonidentically bounded variables gives

\[
P\left(
|\bar h_r-E\bar h_r|>\delta_r
\mid D
\right)
\le\alpha/R.
\]

The union bound puts the true average expectation in \(B_n\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha\). The true nuisance places that expectation in \(\mathcal M_h(u_0)\); any moment-equivalent \(u\) is retained on the same event. \(\square\)

The cluster feature may contain all 1320 cells and retention indicators, so within-sweep dependence is unrestricted. For dependent clusters, replace Hoeffding by a proved block, martingale, or mixing concentration theorem. Empirically selecting a cluster length and then treating it as fixed is not covered.

## 11.7 What a GMM inversion theorem does and does not prove

**Proposition 11.7 (inversion implication for uniformly calibrated tests).**  
If statistics and critical values satisfy

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{(u,\nu)\in\Gamma_n}
P_{u,\nu}\{T_n(u)>c_{n,\alpha}(u)\}
\le\alpha,
\tag{11.14}
\]

then \(\{u:T_n(u)\le c_{n,\alpha}(u)\}\) is uniformly asymptotically honest.

**Proof.** Noncoverage of the true \(u\) is the rejection event in (11.14). \(\square\)

This proposition is an inversion lemma; it assumes the required uniform rejection bound. It is not a bootstrap theorem. To prove (11.14) for LP would require a precise triangular array, fixed or uniformly controlled cluster construction, uniform moment bounds, stochastic equicontinuity, covariance-rank stability, nuisance-graph compactness, treatment of weak identification, and reproduction of state/selection/calibration estimation. No such LP-specific theorem is supplied.

## 11.8 Quantization, nuisance refitting, and selection in simulation

**Theorem 11.8 (full-pipeline simple-null simulation).**  
Under a fully specified simple null, quantization, deterministic preprocessing, nuisance refitting inside the statistic, and data-dependent selection do not invalidate Theorem 11.3 if every observed and simulated complete dataset is passed through the identical measurable pipeline and the reported experiment is the one being simulated.

**Proof.** Exchangeable complete datasets remain exchangeable after the same measurable map is applied componentwise. \(\square\)

This theorem does not validate plug-in generation at \(\widehat\nu(O)\), because the observed dataset and simulations are not generally exchangeable under that random law. Uniform plug-in validity requires a separate theorem.

**Counterexample 11.8.1 (selection omitted).**  
Let \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), but retain/analyze only when \(Z>0\). The selected law is half-normal. A simulator that ignores selection uses the nominal one-sided 5% cutoff 1.645, while

\[
P(Z>1.645\mid Z>0)=0.10.
\]

The true conditional size is 10%. Rerunning selection in unconditional simulations and conditioning on retained replicates, or simulating the exact conditional law, restores the experiment.

Fixing the observed mask, regime gate, association, or state path in simulations is valid only when the target theorem explicitly conditions on those variables and their conditioning is legitimate.

## 11.9 Boundaries, weak identification, disconnected and empty sets

* Boundary maxima and flat profiles require no chi-square approximation under exact or certified least-favorable calibration.
* Near observational equivalence, power must collapse. A full-domain set is a valid inferential result.
* An empty inverted set means every candidate null was rejected. Under a correct model its probability is at most \(\alpha\), but it signals global model conflict or numerical error, not nonidentification.
* A prefit regime failure should return “no model-based claim” or a full set under a declared conditional procedure, not an empty set labeled nonidentifiable.
* Grid inversion covers only grid points unless interval boxes and continuity bounds certify the continuum. Nonfinite optimizer output must retain a candidate, not reject it.
* Randomized p-values can attain exact nominal size under a simple discrete null, but nonrandomized upper-tail p-values are already finite-sample valid and are preferred for auditability.

# 12. Nuisance, dependence, selection, and calibration treatment

## 12.1 Nuisance taxonomy and feasible graph

Write

\[
\nu=(\beta,\eta_{\rm state},\eta_{\rm resp},\eta_{\rm loss},
\eta_{\rm dep},\eta_{\rm sel},\gamma).
\tag{12.1}
\]

The observed state components \((D,E)\) are part of \(O\), not nuisance parameters, although their joint distribution may contain nuisance. The components are:

* \(\beta\): plasma, mirror, beam, background, and physical energy-location nuisance;
* \(S^{\rm lat}\): unobserved format, command, timing, or processor state;
* \(\eta_{\rm resp}\): exposure, energy/angular response, anode efficiency, geometric response;
* \(\eta_{\rm loss}\): dead time, FIFO, rejection, masks, saturation, prescale, arithmetic;
* \(\eta_{\rm dep}\): Cox, renewal, cluster, or other dependence parameters;
* \(\eta_{\rm sel}\): retention and missingness mechanism; and
* \(\gamma\): ground-calibration map and uncertainty.

The allowed values form the feasible graph \(\Gamma\), which must include cross-component restrictions. Averaging over nuisance is frequentist only when a repeated-sampling mixing law is part of the model. Otherwise use conditioning on proved-exogenous variables, profiling, bounding, or least-favorable maximization. A Bayesian prior average is a separate sensitivity analysis.

## 12.2 Observed state, latent state, and endogeneity

Unknown discrete state is not a standard error. If a finite set of latent state histories is compatible with the bytes, include every one in the nuisance set or sum over them under a stated transition law. If the state changes byte layout or code inverse sets and no finite compatible set is known, the observable law is undefined and the route is D.

A recorded state variable may be conditioned on only when it is externally commanded/ancillary for the count law or when the conditional law is derived from a joint model. Automatic feedback flags must be simulated jointly with counts. This applies to prescale, rate-triggered masks, dead-time flags, and FIFO diagnostics whenever their values depend on current or adjacent activity.

Raw/calibrated association selected by maximizing shape correlation is also a data-dependent discrete operation. Valid treatments are independent timing identification, nuisance maximization over plausible associations, data splitting with justified independence, or rerunning the association algorithm in every complete null simulation. The 1998 timing offset is a diagnostic, not a proof of event-time identity.

## 12.3 Response and physical nuisance

A useful response parameterization ties cells and states:

\[
\mu_i(u,\nu)=
D_i(\eta_{\rm eff})
R_i(\eta_E,\eta_\Omega)
\lambda_i(u,\beta)+b_i.
\tag{12.2}
\]

Nonnegativity, normalization where physical, common anode factors, shared state evolution, smooth kernels, and calibration anchors belong in the parameter space. A 1320-dimensional free scale vector returns to Theorem 6.1.

Physical nuisance must also be anchored. A known detector matrix does not prevent Theorem 6.2 if \(\beta\) contains a freely translating energy location. The response package therefore needs either an independently measured plasma-location parameter, a bounded location error, or a nuisance-eliminating contrast proved injective in \(U\).

There are two legitimate frequentist uncertainty routes:

1. a deterministic, source-backed envelope \(\mathcal E\), treated as a maintained restriction; or
2. calibration data and an honest simultaneous region satisfying (11.4).

Bounds selected to produce a desired interval width, a covariance matrix without a sampling interpretation, or a point estimate treated as exact do not propagate instrument uncertainty.

## 12.4 Dependence and cluster definition

The minimum natural cluster is the full 15-row by 88-cell sweep. A visit-level cluster is required if adjacent sweeps share plasma state, instrument accumulation, association, or normalization. Cluster boundaries should be fixed from acquisition timing/state and a prespecified scientific window, not chosen to minimize standard errors after residual inspection.

A cluster sandwich estimates arbitrary within-cluster covariance only under the declared across-cluster law. Serial dependence requires a prespecified block/HAC or martingale treatment and a theorem for the block sequence. Fifteen energy rows are never 15 independent replicates.

Shared-effect likelihoods must include their full dependence. A composite product of marginal interval probabilities may be a working estimating criterion, but its likelihood-ratio calibration must come from the complete cluster law.

## 12.5 Missingness, sentinels, and the sampling frame

Missing constants must be recognized before positivity filters, logarithms, row sums, gates, and normalization. In the bundled 3-D products, `9.999E-99` is a missing sentinel despite being finite and positive. An all-sentinel spectrum with a parsed 1970 time is not a physical low-flux observation.

When opportunity counts and rejection indicators are available, they are included in \(O\). If retention is ignorable conditional on observed design \(W\), with known or honestly estimated \(\pi(W)>0\), inverse-probability moments are possible. Outcome-dependent dead-time masks and flux gates are not ignorable by default. Unrestricted selection invokes Theorem 6.8 even when retention counts are observed.

If only retained records exist, the project may analyze the conditional experiment (3.4), but must not interpret its confidence set as unconditional over all opportunities.

## 12.6 Regime gates and selective claims

A gate based exclusively on externally observed state can define the design stratum. A gate based on the same codes/flux used to fit \(U\) is selection. A valid statement is one of:

* unconditional, with gate pass/fail included as an outcome and rerun in simulation;
* conditional on passing, using the exact conditional law;
* selective, with an explicitly selection-adjusted test; or
* no model-based claim when the physics model is declared inapplicable.

Fixing the observed gate in a bootstrap while simulating unselected data changes the experiment. A failed gate is not an empty confidence set and is not proof of nonidentification.

## 12.7 Calibrated-flux covariance and quasi-likelihood

Calibrated flux inherits covariance from digitization, response calibration, dead-time correction, backgrounds, row normalization, sequential energy timing, and shared state. A diagonal variance such as \((\sigma_{\rm rel}p)^2+\sigma_{\rm abs}^2\) is a working assumption unless traced to calibration data.

If a Gaussian law is maintained, its full covariance and selection support must be modeled. If only a mean restriction is maintained, use cluster estimating equations and a finite-sample moment bound or a separately proved uniform asymptotic theorem. A single sweep does not estimate repeated-cluster covariance.

For normalized angular compositions, covariance is singular on the all-ones direction. Use the invariant tangent-space statistic of Theorem 10.1 or a correct logistic-coordinate law. Robust standard errors do not repair an unidentified mean map.

## 12.8 Contamination and robust neighborhoods

For

\[
P=(1-\epsilon)P_{u,\nu}^0+\epsilon H,
\]

with arbitrary \(H\), the worst-case probability of an event \(A\) is

\[
\sup_H P(A)=(1-\epsilon)P_{u,\nu}^0(A)+\epsilon.
\tag{12.3}
\]

The robust tail probability must then be maximized over nuisance. The radius \(\epsilon\) must be externally justified. Proposition 6.9 locates the overlap boundary; a common overlap invokes Theorem 6.10.

## 12.9 Monte Carlo, adaptive covers, and common random numbers

All simulation randomness is part of the coverage probability. The primary certificate uses fixed budgets and a fixed nuisance cover. Adaptive cover refinement is valid only under a predeclared countable tree with summable failure probabilities or an anytime-valid process. Optimizer failure retains \(u\).

Common random numbers are optional for validity but useful for nuisance comparisons. A counter-based key can be

\[
(\text{master seed},u\text{-box},\text{nuisance node},
\text{replicate},\text{latent stream}).
\]

The same latent uniforms should be transformed under paired model variants. PR 14's floor/no-floor comparison uses different seeds and should be repaired. `[Bundle: pr14/scripts/diagnostics/r_bound_artifact_check.py, lines 302-317]`

# 13. Numerical implementation and certification

## 13.1 Primary conditional method: exact code-space composite-null inversion

This is implementable only after Section 17 supplies a joint observation law and physical anchor. It is not an algorithm for the current unresolved bundle.

### Pseudocode

```text
INPUT
    raw merged records and labels;
    declared sampling frame and opportunity count N_opp;
    physical forward family f(U,beta) with an anchor excluding translation confounding;
    joint state/accepted-count/selection model P_{U,nu};
    deterministic compressor maps q_s, including terminal rules;
    deterministic response envelope or calibration-data nuisance procedure;
    confidence error alpha; numerical and Monte Carlo budgets;
    master counter-based RNG seed.

A. Build the exact observable
    1. Verify file hashes, record lengths, merge/status flags, and source epoch.
    2. Decode proved-exogenous design D and recorded potentially endogenous state E.
    3. Enumerate compatible latent state S_lat; do not place S_lat in the observed data.
    4. Reconstruct the opportunity frame and retention indicators R where available.
       If unavailable, declare the conditional-retained experiment explicitly.
    5. Assemble complete 15 x 88 sweeps and preserve row timing/order.
    6. For each complete state construct I_s(c). Use factor-eight expansion only
       when applicability and integer arithmetic are documented. Treat unknown
       terminal mapping as nuisance or return NO MODEL-BASED CLAIM.

B. Define and validate the observable law
    7. Fold the physical model through exposure, response, efficiency, background,
       masks, dead time/FIFO, accumulator arithmetic, compressor, ground map,
       missingness, and selection.
    8. If E or R is count-generated, evaluate/simulate their joint law with K.
       Condition only on proved-exogenous D.
    9. Check probability normalization, limiting cases, state transitions, and
       reference records. Validate the physical anchor contrast globally.

C. Define the test statistic for H0: U = u
   10. Compute a feasible lower bound L_alt <= sup_{v,xi} ell(v,xi;O).
   11. Compute a certified upper bound U_null >= sup_{nu in H_u} ell(u,nu;O).
   12. Set T_alg(u;O) = 2 max{0, L_alt - U_null}.
       If optimization is unresolved, set T_alg=0. This defines the statistic;
       it does not certify feasibility or identification.

D. Calibrate the generating-null nuisance
   13. Use all H_u, or an honest Berger-Boos region B_u with error gamma.
   14. Predeclare a finite nuisance cover B_j with representatives nu_j and
       certified TV bounds epsilon_j, or a countable refinement tree with a
       summable failure budget.
   15. At each representative simulate a fixed predeclared number of complete
       null datasets. Apply steps A-C identically, including missingness,
       association, gates, selection, nuisance refitting, grid refinement,
       optimizer failures, and quantization.
   16. Form exact one-sided binomial upper bounds U_j at allocated delta_j.
   17. p_cert(u) = min{1, max_j(U_j + epsilon_j) + sum_j delta_j + gamma}.
       Sequential looks are allowed only with alpha spending or an anytime-valid
       confidence sequence.

E. Invert on the continuum
   18. Partition the admissible U domain into closed boxes.
   19. Exclude a box only if a certified upper bound on p_cert(u) is <= alpha
       for every u in the box. Retain every unresolved box.
   20. Refine boundary boxes to the declared display tolerance without replacing
       the box guarantee by interpolation.
   21. Return the union of retained closed boxes, full-domain/no-claim flags,
       nuisance assumptions, calibration status, hashes, seed/substreams,
       optimization bounds, and Monte Carlo certificates.
```

### Conservative statistic

Let

\[
A(O)=\sup_{v,\xi}\ell(v,\xi;O),
\qquad
N_u(O)=\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal H_u}\ell(u,\nu;O).
\]

If \(L_{\rm alt}\le A\) and \(U_{\rm null}\ge N_u\), then

\[
T_{\rm alg}(u;O)=2[L_{\rm alt}-U_{\rm null}]_+
\le2[A-N_u]_+.
\tag{13.1}
\]

The relevant validity argument is not that this lower statistic is a bound on a standard likelihood-ratio law. It is that \(T_{\rm alg}\) is a measurable statistic applied identically to observed and simulated data and calibrated under its own null distribution. Setting it to zero on failure can only reduce rejection.

### Nuisance and Monte Carlo certificate

Theorem 11.4 applies directly. For a nuisance box \(B_j\),

\[
\sup_{\nu\in B_j}
P_{u,\nu}\{T_{\rm alg}(O^*)\ge t\}
\le
P_{u,\nu_j}\{T_{\rm alg}(O^*)\ge t\}
+\epsilon_j.
\tag{13.2}
\]

A fixed-budget one-sided exact binomial upper bound controls the representative tail. The sum of simulation failure probabilities and Berger-Boos \(\gamma\) is added to the p-value or subtracted from the rejection threshold. Ordinary fixed-level Clopper-Pearson bounds may not be repeatedly inspected.

### Nuisance parameterization and global optimization

* Enumerate discrete state, association, and format alternatives.
* Use log coordinates for positive scales/backgrounds, logits for probabilities, simplex coordinates for response kernels, and Cholesky factors for covariance.
* Enforce cross-cell/state calibration ties. Free per-cell scales invoke Theorem 6.1.
* The feasible graph must be closed and bounded or have an analytic tail reduction.
* Use conditional convexity where available, bracketed one-dimensional solvers for scales, and interval branch-and-bound or verified relaxations for nonconvex nuisance.
* Multistart local optimization supplies feasible lower bounds only. It never supplies a global upper bound.
* For the null likelihood, an unresolved global upper bound makes \(T_{\rm alg}=0\). For the alternative, an unresolved lower bound can use any feasible value.
* Never shrink the nuisance domain because the optimizer failed.

### Stable interval probabilities

For finite Poisson intervals use (7.3)-(7.6), switching between log-CDF and log-survival representations. For terminal saturation use the documented survival or finite interval; if terminal behavior is unresolved, do not substitute an upper tail. Shared-Gamma quadrature is accumulated in the log domain with certified quadrature error. Probability-mass normalization across all codes is tested for each state and parameter boundary.

### Continuum and grid certification

A grid produces a grid confidence set only. To cover continuous \(U\), maintain boxes and bounds on likelihood/statistic/p-value variation over each box. Exclude only when every point is certified rejected. Linear interpolation of profile crossings is display-only and cannot remove retained boxes.

## 13.2 Correct feasibility and identification certificates

For a partial-identification residual \(\rho(u)\), exclusion requires a certified lower bound \(\underline\rho(u)>0\). A zero lower bound is inconclusive. A feasible zero-residual witness proves compatibility. The numerical outer set is therefore all boxes not certified infeasible.

For identification, define

\[
\Delta_\varepsilon=
\inf_{\substack{(u,\nu),(v,\xi)\in\Gamma\\|u-v|\ge\varepsilon}}
 d\{P_{u,\nu}^O,P_{v,\xi}^O\}.
\tag{13.3}
\]

The valid certificates are:

* \(\underline\Delta_\varepsilon>0\): uniform separation proved;
* an exact feasible pair with equal laws: nonidentification proved;
* a certified upper bound numerically small but positive: evidence of weak identification, not equivalence;
* \(\underline\Delta_\varepsilon=0\): no conclusion.

When laws are on a finite code space and exactly evaluable, equality can be certified by matching every mass. For continuous/numerical laws, an analytic equivalence construction is preferable.

## 13.3 Best fallback: bounded-feature moment outer set

```text
INPUT
    complete observed clusters O_i including R/state where available;
    exogenous design values D_i;
    predeclared bounded features h_ir(O_i) in [a_ir,b_ir];
    source-backed response/selection expectation set M_h(u);
    alpha and U-domain tolerance.

1. Fix clusters and the common/regression estimand before residual inspection.
2. Apply source-backed missingness and state rules; include R as a feature when
   the opportunity frame is observed.
3. Compute the nonidentical-cluster Hoeffding widths in (11.11).
4. For each U-box, solve whether some u and nuisance produce an average expected
   feature vector in B_n.
5. Exclude only when a positive global infeasibility bound is certified.
6. Retain unresolved boxes and return an outer region for the moment-compatible
   set, with the maintained response envelope and cluster law stated.
```

Useful features include code-threshold indicators, bounded angular contrasts, missingness/retention indicators, and finite interval endpoints. Terminal-code indicators remain bounded even when the latent terminal mean is not. Feature selection based on the same data must be included in a simultaneous family or calibration.

## 13.4 Reproducible random-number and failure policy

Use master seed `20260722` with counter-based substreams keyed by candidate box, nuisance node, replicate, and latent component. Fixed simulation budgets are recorded before the run. For an alpha-spending refinement, store every permitted look and its \(\delta_{j\ell}\). Common random numbers should be used for paired nuisance/model comparisons but do not replace tail-probability certification.

Every failure is one-sided conservatively:

* unsupported state or unknown terminal map: do not silently drop the record; condition on a predeclared exogenous supported-state stratum or return a full/no-model-based claim for the affected target;
* unresolved nuisance supremum: retain \(u\);
* unresolved feasibility residual: retain \(u\);
* nonfinite profile value: retain, not reject;
* Monte Carlo decision unresolved at budget cap: retain;
* component count beyond storage schema: preserve dynamically or return a full envelope, never truncate silently.

## 13.5 Complexity

Let \(n\) be clusters, \(d=1320\), \(G\) target boxes, \(J\) nuisance boxes, \(B\) simulations per representative, and \(Q\) quadrature nodes. Independent-count likelihood evaluation is \(O(nd)\); shared-Gamma is \(O(Qnd)\). Direct certified inversion is roughly

\[
O\{GJ B\times(\text{fit cost})\},
\]

plus global optimization and cover refinement. The moment fallback costs \(O(nR)\) for features and the feasibility-solver cost per target box. Computational expense justifies coarser conservative bounds, not plug-in nuisance or invalid optional stopping.

# 14. Simulation and coverage-validation plan

Simulation has four distinct roles. They must not be merged.

## 14.1 Layer I: deterministic implementation tests

These tests compare code against quantities known independently of the fitted model.

1. Enumerate compressor inverse intervals for every nonterminal code and every documented prescale state.
2. Verify terminal behavior only for states with an authoritative rule; unsupported states must fail closed.
3. Compare Poisson, negative-binomial, and renewal interval probabilities with direct high-precision summation for small/moderate means and with complementary-tail identities for large means.
4. Verify probability normalization over all 256 codes, including terminal mass.
5. Check shared-Gamma quadrature against direct numerical integration in low-dimensional cases.
6. Check permutation invariance of the simplex statistic and deliberate noninvariance of the diagonal dropped-cell counterexample.
7. Check that sentinel, missingness, selection, and optimizer failures are reproduced identically in observed and simulated pipelines.

These tests establish software identities, not instrument truth.

## 14.2 Layer II: identification proof or global certificate

Every A classification requires one of:

* an analytic anchor-contrast proof such as Theorem 5.3;
* an exact equality witness proving nonidentification; or
* a certified global lower bound \(\underline\Delta_\varepsilon>0\) for every scientifically relevant \(\varepsilon\).

A numerical search that fails to find an equivalent pair is not a proof. A zero lower bound is not evidence of equivalence. A small feasible distance is a weak-identification diagnostic only. The search domain must be the closed feasible nuisance graph, including physical location nuisance, response bounds, state alternatives, and selection.

For a finite code law, a global certificate can minimize total variation or Hellinger distance using interval/global optimization. For simulator-only laws, use analytic coupling bounds or a validated emulator with a rigorous error envelope; ordinary Monte Carlo estimates of distance cannot certify exact disjointness.

## 14.3 Layer III: repeated-sampling calibration audit

For a procedure whose theorem is already established, simulate to detect implementation error and assess width/power. Use master seed `20260722` and predeclared fixed replication counts. A minimum scenario grid includes:

1. ordinary interior parameters with moderate code occupancy;
2. \(U\) and nuisance boundaries;
3. weak anchor contrasts and near-overlap law sets;
4. extensive zero codes and terminal codes;
5. response-envelope boundary points and least-favorable nuisance corners;
6. specified shared-Gamma, negative-binomial, renewal, and FIFO alternatives;
7. endogenous state/feedback generated jointly with counts;
8. missingness and selection both under the maintained mechanism and under controlled misspecification;
9. physical translation confounding to verify that an A procedure becomes full/unresolved when its anchor is removed;
10. calibration-region failure events at their allocated \(\gamma\); and
11. optimizer, quadrature, and simulation-budget failures, which must widen rather than narrow the set.

For each fixed generating law report noncoverage, full-domain frequency, empty-set frequency, number of components, Lebesgue/grid width, boundary error, optimizer failures, and Monte Carlo certification failures. Binomial uncertainty on simulated coverage is reported with a fixed replication count or a separately alpha-spent sequential design. It is not called proof of uniform coverage.

A finite nuisance grid checks only the simulated points. Least-favorable claims require the analytic/numerical nuisance certificate in Section 13.

## 14.4 Layer IV: empirical falsification and transfer diagnostics

The bundled 1998 and 1999 records can stress assumptions:

* compare code histograms, zero/terminal rates, and cross-cell dependence by decoded state;
* test stability of response-normalized shapes across repeated state strata;
* compare raw-code-derived summaries with independently produced low/high-resolution products;
* inspect rate/FIFO/dead-time diagnostics against code saturation and missingness;
* assess whether proposed shared-effect covariance is rank-one/low-rank or cell-specific;
* hold out epochs when fitting any response emulator and report transfer error by state;
* rerun association and gates under perturbations;
* inspect whether physical nuisance estimates track \(U\) through the exact translation direction of Theorem 6.2.

Failure can reject a model. Absence of detected failure does not establish the response envelope, identification, or coverage.

## 14.5 Minimal procedure-validation design

For the primary exact-law method, choose a small state-resolved synthetic experiment where every code probability can be enumerated. Predeclare:

```text
U grid:       {-150, -100, -50, 0, 50} V plus two off-grid values
n clusters:   {1, 5, 25}
responses:    nominal; two calibration-boundary points; one near-equivalent point
counts:       Poisson; Gamma-Cox; NB r in {0.3, 2, 20}; renewal dead time
selection:    external; modeled outcome-dependent; deliberately omitted
state:        exogenous; prior-count-triggered endogenous flag
codes:        ordinary bins; wide bins; terminal saturation
replications: fixed in advance for each cell of the design
```

The exact procedure must attain size at most \(\alpha\) within Monte Carlo reporting error under every maintained-law scenario. Deliberate model violations should be labeled sensitivity/misspecification, not coverage.

For the moment fallback, simulate independent heterogeneous clusters with known bounded feature expectations and arbitrary within-cluster copulas. Verify the simultaneous set-coverage event of Theorem 11.6. Then introduce cross-cluster dependence to demonstrate failure when the concentration premise is violated.

## 14.6 PR 14 validation replacement

The current tests are geometry and smoke tests. Replace them with:

1. sentinel and all-missing record tests before normalization/gating;
2. cell-permutation invariance tests;
3. covariance tests showing normalized proportions are correlated and singular;
4. full-pipeline simulation tests that rerun masks, gates, association, fitting, and grid refinement;
5. fixed-budget or alpha-spent Monte Carlo tests;
6. nuisance-boundary and weak-identification cases;
7. dynamic serialization of arbitrarily many disconnected components; and
8. explicit tests that all numerical failures retain candidates.

Even after these tests pass, the PR remains a working-model sensitivity method until the observation law, response uncertainty, and physical anchor are supplied.

# 17. Exact additional information needed from the TBS/calibration package

## 17.1 Information required to define the observable experiment

By state and epoch, the project needs:

1. **Opportunity frame and retention provenance:** how many 3-D opportunities existed, which were telemetered, which entered each calibrated product, and every exclusion/quality rule; whether rejected records and gate outcomes are recoverable.
2. **Format/state decoder:** authoritative Digital Subcom/software-version mappings, including version 5, byte layout, energy table, command changes, header semantics, and state-transition timing.
3. **State causality:** which states are externally commanded, which are automatic feedback from current/prior rates, and the transition law needed to model endogenous flags jointly with counts.
4. **Compressor terminal rule:** counter width, maximum, saturation/overflow/wrap behavior, exact inverse of `FF`, and explicit applicability/arithmetic of any 3-D prescale.
5. **Event-time and exposure:** cell integration windows from major-frame time, spin phase/period, merge/delayed selection, latency, polar summing, and sequential energy timing.
6. **Accepted-event and accumulator chain:** masks, pulse-height discrimination, backgrounds, dead time, FIFO routing/capacity/service, event rejection, retrace handling, integer precision, summing order, rounding/truncation, and overflow.
7. **Energy response:** state-specific passbands/response matrices, not only center energies or nominal \(\Delta E/E\).
8. **Angular/anode response:** 256-effective-anode efficiency table, anode-to-cell map, ADC differential nonlinearity, MCP gain/flat field, geometric factors, and cross-state ties.
9. **Ground calibration:** exact code/state-to-flux algorithm, dead-time masking, background treatment, rotations, rounding, sentinels, and product-generation selection.
10. **Cross-product reference cases:** raw records and state that reproduce 3-D calibrated flux and independent low/high-resolution products.

The bundle explicitly marks the effective-anode table and onboard phase-space arithmetic reference as TBS and references unavailable commanding/ICD material. `[Bundle: KNOWN_FACTS_AND_OPEN_QUESTIONS.md, lines 80-106; primary_sources/magerfmt.txt, lines 4-8, 503-514, and 698-721]`

## 17.2 Information required to propagate instrument uncertainty

One of the following is necessary:

* a deterministic, source-backed coupled envelope for response, loss, timing, terminal, and ground-calibration error; or
* calibration data \(D_{\rm cal}\), a model connecting calibration and flight conditions, and a simultaneous nuisance region satisfying (11.4).

The package must specify covariance/coupling across cells and states. Independent arbitrary cell bounds can be so broad that Theorem 6.1 remains feasible. Model discrepancy between laboratory calibration and flight conditions requires a separate robust envelope/radius.

## 17.3 Information required to identify \(U\) rather than an energy-shift equivalence class

The physical package must supply at least one of:

1. an independently measured plasma energy-location/spacecraft-potential anchor;
2. a source-backed bound on the additive location nuisance narrow enough to leave a proper identified set; or
3. a response-normalized contrast \(A(\mu_J)=\psi(U)\) proved one-to-one over the entire physical nuisance domain.

A detector response package alone does not remove Theorem 6.2. A local optimizer or local Jacobian at one nominal nuisance does not establish global identification.

## 17.4 Operationality and inferential sufficiency propositions

**Proposition 17.1 (information sufficient to define a code-space observation law).**  
Suppose the recovered package provides:

1. the observable sampling frame (3.2), including observed versus latent state and retention;
2. exact compressor inverse sets for every supported state;
3. an evaluable joint mass function or exact simulator for \((E,S^{\rm lat},K,R)\mid D,U,\nu\);
4. the response/loss/ground maps and a declared nuisance graph; and
5. deterministic numerical error bounds for any evaluator/simulator approximation.

Then \(P_{u,\nu}^O\) is operationally defined for every feasible \((u,\nu)\). Exact recovery of latent \(K\) is unnecessary.

**Proof.** Lemma 5.1 pushes the joint kernel through state-dependent compression and retention. Summing/integrating latent state and ground-processing variables gives the distribution of the observed random element. An exact inverse of the lossy code is not required; its interval probability is sufficient. \(\square\)

This proposition establishes evaluability, not identification.

**Proposition 17.2 (additional information sufficient for a conditional A-class confidence set).**  
In addition to Proposition 17.1, suppose:

1. the true response/calibration nuisance lies in a fixed maintained envelope or in an honest region used through Theorem 11.2;
2. the physical model satisfies the anchor condition of Theorem 5.3, or a certified global separation condition of Proposition 5.4;
3. the feasible nuisance graph is compact or has a certified tail reduction;
4. the least-favorable p-value is evaluated exactly or upper-certified by Theorem 11.4 or Proposition 11.5; and
5. continuum inversion uses retained target boxes as in Section 13.

Then the inverted set has finite-sample coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) over the declared class.

**Proof.** The observation law is defined by Proposition 17.1. The anchor/separation condition identifies \(U\); identification is not needed for formal coverage but is needed for a nontrivial target. Theorems 11.1-11.4 and Proposition 11.5 control composite nuisance, calibration-region error, and Monte Carlo randomness. The one-sided optimizer, quadrature, and continuum bounds in Section 13 control numerical approximation. Boxwise conservative inversion can only enlarge the accepted set. \(\square\)

**Proposition 17.3 (information sufficient for a B-class outer set).**  
If full-law evaluability or point identification is unavailable but the package supplies a valid coupled response/loss envelope and replicated-cluster expectation sets for bounded observable features, Theorem 11.6 yields a finite-sample outer confidence region for the moment-compatible set. No accumulator distribution is required.

## 17.5 Acceptable substitute for historical TBS

A state-aware empirical emulator can substitute for historical calibration documents only if it:

1. predicts the joint observable law, including retention/state, on held-out epochs;
2. ties cells/states strongly enough to defeat response absorption;
3. includes a physical anchor excluding translation confounding;
4. supplies a deterministic or calibration-data uncertainty envelope covering transfer error;
5. reproduces raw-to-3-D and independent aggregate products; and
6. is used as an empirical observation model, not mislabeled as recovered detector physics.

Without an externally validated error envelope, it remains a point predictor and sensitivity device.

# 21. Limitations, adversarial self-check, and prohibited claims

## 21.1 Adversarial self-check of every positive class

For each A/B class, the following attacks were applied.

### Attack A: hide or endogenize state

Replace an observed design variable by a latent or count-generated flag. If the proposed likelihood conditions on it without a joint model, the claim fails. Repair: use the experiment in Section 3 and model \((E,S^{\rm lat},K,R)\) jointly. This attack defeats any current LP A claim because state causality is incomplete.

### Attack B: absorb \(U\) into response

Allow independent cell scales/backgrounds. Theorem 6.1 then gives exact equivalence. Repair: known ratios, low-dimensional tied response, anchors, or narrow coupled bounds. No positive class permits arbitrary cellwise response.

### Attack C: absorb \(U\) into physical energy location

Allow \(f_j\) to depend on \(U+\theta\) with unrestricted \(\theta\). Theorem 6.2 defeats identification even with known detector response. Repair: independent anchor, bounded \(\theta\), or an anchor contrast.

### Attack D: collapse stochastic information

For shared mixing, place all mass at zero; for negative-binomial let shape approach zero; for contamination include a common law. Exact common laws defeat identification, while boundary-approaching sequences defeat uniform distinguishability without necessarily invalidating every finite-sample least-favourable set. Repair: distinguish the two conclusions, impose positivity/information lower bounds for uniform usefulness, and keep robust radii below the overlap boundary.

### Attack E: match selection including retention

Allow outcome-dependent selection and match the full \((R,RO)\) law by Theorem 6.8. Repair: known external gate, joint selection model, or bounded inclusion probabilities. Merely observing how many records were retained is not enough under unrestricted selection.

### Attack F: exploit joint dependence

Hold marginal Poisson means fixed while changing the copula/dependence. This defeats sharpness of a mean-only identified set. Repair: specify the joint law or report a marginal/moment outer set.

### Attack G: exploit vector calibration structure

Coordinatewise monotone calibration cannot erase copula differences, so the former scalar transport proof was overbroad. Repair: narrow the theorem to marginal claims or explicitly allow a joint randomized kernel. No revised conclusion relies on coordinatewise monotonicity to prove full-vector equivalence.

### Attack H: approach nonregular points

Set the first derivative to zero while preserving injectivity, or approach a nuisance boundary where covariance rank changes. Repair: separate identification from regular first-order information and avoid unproved Wald/Wilks/bootstrap limits.

### Attack I: exploit numerical certificate direction

Return zero lower bounds from branch-and-bound. Such output cannot prove feasibility or equivalence. Repair: positive lower bound for exclusion/separation; equality witness for compatibility/equivalence; otherwise retain.

### Attack J: optional stopping in Monte Carlo

Repeatedly inspect fixed-look binomial bounds. The false-certificate rate exceeds the nominal allocation. Repair: fixed budgets, alpha spending, or anytime-valid confidence sequences.

After these attacks, the surviving positive results are conditional mathematical theorems only. None establishes the present LP archive assumptions.

## 21.2 Claims the project must not make

The project must not state that:

1. decompressed lower endpoints are accepted counts;
2. rounded inverse calibrated flux is archived \(K\);
3. a counter plus an average rate implies independent Poisson sampling;
4. any recorded state may be conditioned on while leaving the count law unchanged;
5. known detector response alone identifies \(U\);
6. local rank, profile curvature, or a unique optimizer proves global identification;
7. compactness of an arbitrary mixing-law class prevents all-zero degeneration;
8. Poisson marginal means make a bounded-response set sharp for the full joint law;
9. coordinatewise monotone calibration erases every multivariate distinction;
10. a retained conditional equivalence proves equivalence of the full sampling frame without matching retention;
11. robust standard errors repair response or physical nonidentification;
12. profiling nuisance supplies composite-null coverage;
13. a plug-in bootstrap is exact because it refits nuisance;
14. fixed-level Clopper-Pearson intervals remain valid under optional stopping;
15. zero optimization lower bounds certify feasibility or nonidentification;
16. finite self-model simulations establish identification or uniform coverage;
17. a deterministic engineering range has frequentist calibration coverage unless adopted explicitly as a maintained envelope;
18. a full-domain or disconnected set is an algorithm failure;
19. an empty set means nonidentification; or
20. PR 14 currently provides an honest confidence set.

## 21.3 Remaining limitations of the revised memo

The positive theorems are mathematically complete under their declared assumptions, but several scientific choices remain unresolved:

* the correct physical nuisance parameterization for plasma distribution and spacecraft-relative energy shift;
* whether any low-dimensional response/anchor model is defensible for flight data;
* whether a specified shared-effect, renewal, or FIFO law fits the accepted-event chain;
* whether enough repeated stable-state clusters exist for nontrivial moment inference;
* how narrow a source-backed calibration envelope can be made; and
* whether a feasible global law-separation certificate can be computed at 1320-cell scale.

The empirical audit uses two reference periods and code behavior. It cannot recover missing TBS material or prove the observation model. The decision matrix therefore distinguishes unresolved D classes from exact C impossibility classes.

## Final decision

**The case record does not currently justify a nontrivial honest per-sweep confidence set for \(U\).** The raw-code and intended calibrated-flux routes are D because the exact observable experiment, response/calibration uncertainty, accepted-count law, and physical anchor are missing. Broader classes that allow arbitrary response, physical translation, vector mixing, selection, or support only are C by explicit equivalence theorems.

Useful inference remains mathematically possible under assumptions weaker than independent Poisson, but those assumptions must be explicit and source-backed. The primary conditional A route is exact code-space least-favorable inversion under a specified joint state/count law and an anchor contrast. The primary B fallback is a finite-sample bounded-feature outer set under a validated response/selection expectation envelope and genuine replicated clusters.

# 24. Remaining unproved claims and unresolved source facts

## 24.1 Mathematical results not proved for the current LP experiment

The following statements remain unproved and are not used as established conclusions:

1. **Global LP physical identification.** No source-backed continuous response-folded forward model has been shown to possess an anchor contrast \(A(\mu)=\psi(U)\) over the full plasma nuisance domain.
2. **Positive global separation.** No certified \(\Delta(\varepsilon)>0\) has been computed for any current LP nuisance class after quantization, state, response, and selection.
3. **Nonparametric shared-mixing injectivity.** It is unresolved whether a positive mixing distribution bounded away from zero is identifiable jointly with \(U\) from the finite coded sweep law under a defensible response model.
4. **LP cluster bootstrap validity.** No pointwise or uniform theorem covers the actual profiled, quantized, selected, nuisance-refitted statistic with data-dependent covariance rank and cluster construction.
5. **Across-sweep dependence.** No finite-sample concentration or uniform mixing theorem has been verified for the intended pooled LP sweep/visit sequence.
6. **Nontrivial moment separation.** No bounded observable feature set with source-backed expectation envelopes has been shown to separate scientifically relevant \(U\) values in the bundled data.
7. **Robust-neighborhood radius.** No externally calibrated total-variation, contamination, Wasserstein, or other discrepancy radius has been justified for LP flight response/model error.
8. **Practical global optimization certificate.** It is not yet shown that the 1320-cell nuisance supremum and continuum inversion can be certified at feasible cost without excessive conservatism.
9. **Emulator transfer theorem.** No empirical raw-to-product emulator has a simultaneous held-out error envelope connected to flight-state coverage.
10. **Selective/conditional gate law.** No exact conditional law has been derived for the PR 14 regime gates or product-generation selection.
